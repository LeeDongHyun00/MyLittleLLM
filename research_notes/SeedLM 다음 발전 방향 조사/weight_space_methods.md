# Weight-space methods for combining, compressing and extending what small from-scratch models have learned (task arithmetic, merging, weight averaging, ensemble distillation, continual injection)

Research notes, compiled 2026-11-23. Scope: the literature up to mid/late 2026, read for one question: do these methods work for SMALL transformers trained FROM SCRATCH (here: 12.4M-parameter program-writing "coders", about 20 of them, plus three families of paired 400-step fine-tunes), and what should be expected if they are tried.

## 1. Task arithmetic: definitions, reported gains, and the conditions under which it works (including evidence for small or from-scratch models and for fine-tunes of only a few hundred steps)

### Takeaway
Task arithmetic (add, negate, or take analogies of "fine-tuned minus base" weight differences) is well established only for pre-trained models fine-tuned from one shared checkpoint: there, eight CLIP task vectors add up to about 91% of the single-task accuracy, and negation removes a behaviour (-45.8 points on the target task) at a small cost elsewhere. The property that makes it work ("weight disentanglement") has so far been shown to come from pre-training: it is absent at random initialisation, weaker in smaller and weaker base models, and I found no direct test on ~10M-parameter models trained from scratch. Two things are favourable for the present setting: vectors from only a few hundred fine-tuning steps merge as well as or better than long ones, and the fine-tunes share a base. One thing caps the upside: to first order a sum of task vectors is one step of joint (multi-task) training, so it approximates, and normally does not beat, a fine-tune that uses all ingredients together.

### Cited Findings

#### Definitions and headline results (Ilharco et al. 2023, "Editing Models with Task Arithmetic")
- Definitions: task vector tau_t = theta_ft(t) - theta_pre; edited model theta_new = theta + lambda * tau_new, where "the scaling term is determined using held-out validation sets"; negation uses -tau; addition uses the sum of vectors; analogy "A is to B as C is to D" uses tau_C + (tau_B - tau_A). **[PT-large] (full)** — [Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)
- Negation, CLIP (target-task accuracy / control = ImageNet accuracy): ViT-L/14 pre-trained 64.8 / 75.5 -> negative task vector 19.0 / 72.9 (target -45.8 points, control -2.6); ViT-B/32 48.3 / 63.4 -> 24.0 / 60.9; ViT-B/16 55.2 / 68.3 -> 21.3 / 65.4. Baselines for ViT-L/14: fine-tuning by gradient ascent 3.93 / 16.3 (destroys the control task); a random vector of the same kind 60.9 / 72.9 (almost no effect on the target). The negation coefficient is the largest lambda that keeps at least 95% of control accuracy. **[PT-large] (full)** — [Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)
- Negation, language model (GPT-2 Large; vector from fine-tuning on toxic text): toxic generations 4.8% -> 0.8%, mean toxicity score 0.06 -> 0.01, WikiText-103 perplexity 16.4 -> 16.9. Baselines: fine-tuning on non-toxic text 1.8% / 0.03 / 17.2; gradient ascent 0.0% toxic but perplexity above 10^10; random vector 4.8% / 0.06 / 16.4. The paper reports the same trend for GPT-2 Medium (1.8% toxic, perplexity 18.9) and GPT-2 Small (2.5%, perplexity 25.3). **[PT-large / PT-small] (full)** — [Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)
- Addition: adding pairs of CLIP task vectors gives "98.9% normalized accuracy on average"; adding all eight gives a best average of 91.2% normalized accuracy. A single lambda from {0, 0.05, ..., 1.0} scales the sum; "coefficients in the range 0.3 to 0.5 produce close to optimal results in many cases"; tuning one coefficient per vector helped "by 0.7 percentage points on average" but needed 10^3 instead of 10 evaluations; "the variance in the optimal scaling coefficients can be large, highlighting the need for tuning on a case-by-case basis". **[PT-large] (full)** — [Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)
- Addition is weaker than joint training: multi-task fine-tuning reaches 0.994 normalized accuracy "compared to the best result obtained with task vectors, 0.912". **[PT-large] (full)** — [Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)
- Adding outside task vectors to an already fine-tuned language model gives small gains: T5-base on GLUE, zero-shot / fine-tuned / fine-tuned + task vectors: average 57.1 / 78.1 / 78.6 (MRPC 88.5 -> 89.3, RTE 77.3 -> 77.5, CoLA 52.3 -> 53.0, SST-2 94.5 -> 94.7). **[PT-large] (full)** — [Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)
- Analogies: sentiment analysis without target labels (auxiliary fine-tuned -> with analogy vs. fine-tuned on the target): Yelp, T5-small 88.6 -> 89.9 (target-trained 91.1), T5-base 92.3 -> 93.0 (93.4), T5-large 95.0 -> 95.1 (95.5); Amazon, T5-small 87.9 -> 89.0 (90.2). Sub-population analogies improve accuracy "by 3.4 percentage points on average". This is the smallest pre-trained model (T5-small, about 60M parameters **(memory)** for the size) for which I found positive task-arithmetic numbers. **[PT-small / PT-large] (full)** — [Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)
- Relation to ensembles: adding two vectors with lambda = 0.5 equals a uniform weight average of the two fine-tuned models, and its accuracy "closely follows the accuracy of the corresponding ensemble" (Pearson correlation 0.99). **[PT-large] (full)** — [Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)
- Conditions stated by the authors: (a) "in all of our experiments we perform arithmetic operations only on models fine-tuned from the same pre-trained initialization", and "task vectors are restricted to models with the same architecture"; (b) vectors from different tasks "are typically close to orthogonal"; (c) "intermediate task vectors converge rapidly to the direction of the final task vector" and the accuracy from adding them "saturates after just a few hundred steps" (out of 2,000 fine-tuning iterations); (d) higher learning rates hurt task-vector accuracy faster than they hurt the individual models, so the authors "recommend more caution when using task vectors"; (e) five fine-tunes per task differing only in random seed gave edited models of similar accuracy ("different random seeds have little impact"), with the caveat that "we would observe larger variance in other settings such as natural language processing". **[PT-large] (full)** — [Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)

#### Why it works: weight disentanglement and the role of pre-training (Ortiz-Jimenez et al. 2023)
- Weight disentanglement: the function of the edited model decomposes into per-task parts, f(x; theta_0 + sum_t alpha_t tau_t) = sum_t g_t(x; alpha_t tau_t) + g_0(x), where each g_t vanishes outside the input region of its own task; the "disentanglement error" xi(alpha_1, alpha_2) measures the prediction mismatch; "weight disentanglement is a property of the predictors and not related to the performance on different tasks". **[theory + PT-large] (full)** — [Ortiz-Jimenez et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827)
- Pre-training is the source: "weight disentanglement of semantically meaningful tasks is an emergent property of pre-training, as it is absent at random initialization"; "task arithmetic is not achievable on randomly initialized ViTs". Numbers: starting from random initialisation, single-task accuracy after fine-tuning reaches 48.5 / 40.6 / 18.0 (ViT-B/32, B/16, L/14), but the multi-task accuracy after adding the eight task vectors is 5.5 / 4.5 / 4.8, the same as an untrained network (5.3 / 4.8 / 5.2). **[scratch] (full)** — [Ortiz-Jimenez et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827)
- Model size: "larger models are more weight disentangled"; the advantage of linearised fine-tuning "is higher for the smaller ViT-B/32 and progressively diminishes as the model size increases". **[PT-large] (full)** — [Ortiz-Jimenez et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827)
- Linearised (tangent-space) fine-tuning: task addition, absolute / normalised accuracy, non-linear fine-tuning vs. linearised: ViT-B/32 71.4 / 76.5 vs. 76.5 / 85.4; ViT-B/16 75.5 / 80.0 vs. 81.3 / 86.0; ViT-L/14 85.1 / 88.8 vs. 88.5 / 93.5 ("up to 5.8 points more"). Negation (target / control): ViT-B/32 24.0 / 60.7 vs. 10.9 / 60.8; ViT-L/14 18.0 / 72.5 vs. 7.9 / 72.5 ("up to 13.1 points more"). Linearising after ordinary fine-tuning ("post-hoc") loses absolute accuracy (ViT-B/32 addition 57.1 absolute), "a clear sign that fine-tuning has not happened in a linear regime". Cost: two to three times the inference time and about twice the training cost and memory. **[PT-large] (full)** — [Ortiz-Jimenez et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827)
- The pre-training effect is not specific to CLIP, but depends on how the base was pre-trained: with ViT-B/16, normalised task-addition accuracy is 82.0% (supervised ImageNet-1k), 89.3% (supervised ImageNet-21k), 73.0% (MAE), 90.9% (DINO), 94.9% (CLIP LAION-400M); "weight disentanglement is a general consequence of pre-training"; "weight disentanglement strengthens as model sizes increase"; but task addition gives "only a small (2-3%) performance gain over linear probing" for these models, and the influence of pre-training data and algorithm "is yet to be understood". Random initialisation is not studied. **[PT-large] (full)** — [Hazimeh, Favero, Frossard 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14569)
- Base-model quality and size (large LLMs): merging is "more effective when experts are created from strong base models" and "larger models are consistently easier to merge". Normalised held-in performance, averaged over four merging methods, 2 / 4 / 6 / 8 experts: 1B pre-trained base 0.63 / 0.44 / 0.36 / 0.26; 1B instruction-tuned base 0.85 / 0.78 / 0.81 / 0.83; 64B pre-trained base 0.80 / 0.74 / 0.69 / 0.67; 64B instruction-tuned 0.97 / 0.91 / 0.89 / 0.93. "All merging methods exhibit similar performance when merging large instruction-tuned models". **[PT-large] (full)** — [Yadav et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03617)
- The same direction in a 2025 benchmark of 2B-9B Llama and Gemma models, five domains, eight merging methods: "merging tends to perform better on stronger base models"; a gap to multi-task models remains for in-domain performance. **[PT-large] (abs)** — [He et al. 2025, MergeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10833)

#### What a task vector is, to first order: a gradient step (theory published 2025)
- Under full-batch gradient descent, a task vector from one epoch of fine-tuning "is exactly equivalent to the negative gradient of the loss, scaled by the learning rate", so adding task vectors equals one step of multi-task gradient descent; for k > 1 epochs, theta_TA(k) = theta_MT(k) + eta^2 * C + O(eta^3) (the error is second order in the learning rate and bounded for feed-forward networks). Empirically (seven vision benchmarks) the first-epoch gradient dominates the fine-tuning trajectory in norm and direction (cosine similarity above 0.8 with later epochs), and merging models fine-tuned for a single epoch "often performs comparably to merging fully converged models". The analysis does not cover SGD noise or Adam (approximate only). **[theory + PT-large] (full for claims, figures not read)** — [Zhou et al. 2025, "On Task Vectors and Gradients"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16082)
- Task arithmetic "is mathematically equivalent to the commonly used algorithm in Federated Learning", one-shot Federated Averaging; data heterogeneity and training heterogeneity are the two factors that limit it. **[theory + PT-large] (abs)** — [Tao et al. 2024/2025 (TMLR)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18607)
- First generalisation theory for non-linear transformers (single-layer conceptual model; ICLR 2025 oral): addition provably works when the tasks are "irrelevant or aligned"; negation provably unlearns a task when the other tasks are "irrelevant or contradictory"; validated on unlearning with Phi-1.5 (1.3B). **[theory + PT-large] (abs)** — [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10957)
- Compositionality "depends on staying within the pre-training basin" (second-order analysis of non-linear networks; ICLR 2025 spotlight). **[theory + PT-large] (abs)** — [Porrello et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16350)

#### Fine-tuning length and learning rate: short fine-tunes are an advantage
- "Optimal merging occurs well before full convergence, often when experts are less accurate on their original tasks." CLIP ViT-B/32, 8 tasks, full fine-tuning: merged accuracy at 256 steps vs. 2,048 steps: Task Arithmetic 72.7 vs. 69.8, TIES 75.8 vs. 73.8, DARE 72.6 vs. 69.6, although the experts themselves are worse at 256 steps (88.4% vs. 90.0%). With LoRA, merging peaks at 64-128 steps and at 2,048 steps TIES falls to 50.7 (from about 67). T5-base, 7 tasks: Task Arithmetic 73.7 at 256 steps vs. 70.1 at 2,048. Explanation offered: later training memorises the hardest examples (memorisation score of hard examples rises from 0.21 at step 256 to 0.45 at step 2,048) and "over 50% of examples forgotten during merging come from the hardest 30%". An early-stopping recipe (about 270-485 steps for T5) gains roughly 4 points for Task Arithmetic and TIES. The same trend is reported for T5-Large and T5-3B. (v1 title "Less is More: Undertraining Experts Improves Model Upcycling"; v2 "From Memorization to Parameter Interference: How Overtraining Experts Harms Model Merging", ICML 2026.) **[PT-large / PT-small] (full)** — [Horoi et al. 2025/2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14126)
- The best training length depends on the merging method (Qwen 3.5 0.8B/2B/4B, QLoRA experts in five domains, checkpoints from 0.25x to 5x the validation-optimal step count T*): simple averaging and greedy soups are best with under-trained experts (0.25-1.0x T*) and lose "14-23 percentage points on average by 5x T*"; sign/sparsity methods (TIES, DARE+TIES) are best past the optimum (1.5-5x T*), with TIES "exceeding its T* score by 2.5-8.7 percentage points". The authors call their bias-variance account "an explanatory hypothesis rather than a proven mechanism". **[PT-large] (full)** — [Kozodoi, Afolabi, Butler 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11997)
- Optimiser noise matters in a non-monotonic way: an "effective noise scale" (learning rate, weight decay, batch size, augmentation) predicts mergeability, with a distinct optimum; too little or too much noise gives almost no merging benefit (ICLR 2026). **[scratch and PT, not specified in abstract] (abs)** — [Zhang et al. 2025/2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04686)
- Large learning rates create error barriers between fine-tunes of the same checkpoint (see Section 2, model soups). **[PT-large] (full)** — [Wortsman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)

#### Shared initialisation: when do two models trained from the same starting point stay linearly connected?
- For networks trained from scratch, two copies trained from the same random initialisation with different SGD noise are generally NOT linearly connected ("error rises to the point of random guessing when linearly interpolating"); only LeNet on MNIST is stable from initialisation. But they become stable to SGD noise early in training: ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10 after iteration 2,000 (3% of training), VGG-16 after iteration 1,000 (1.5%), ResNet-50 on ImageNet after epoch 18 (20%), Inception-v3 after epoch 28 (16%). Stability threshold used: under 2% error increase along the path. **[scratch] (full)** — [Frankle et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05671)
- Two fine-tunes of the same pre-trained weights have no performance barrier between them, while two models trained from random initialisation do, "even if we use the same random initialization values" (barriers are only "slightly smaller" in that case). **[PT-large vs. scratch] (full)** — [Neyshabur, Sedghi, Zhang 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11687)
- Small networks trained from scratch (two-layer MLPs, a reduced ResNet-18): the solution of sequential (continual) training and the multi-task solution are connected by a linear low-error path "conditioned on having the same initialization for both"; with different initialisations (CIFAR-100) the multi-task minimum "is no longer linearly connected ... and is separated by a wall". **[scratch-small] (full)** — [Mirzadeh et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04495)
- A shared starting point is not a guarantee even for pre-trained models: fine-tuned text classifiers (MNLI, QQP, CoLA) fall into separate linearly connected clusters with barriers between them, and the clusters correspond to different generalisation strategies (ICLR 2023). **[PT-small] (abs)** — [Juneja et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12411)
- Moving a task vector to a base it was not trained from needs extra machinery or shared ancestry: (i) a "chat vector" (chat model minus its base) can be added to a model that was continually pre-trained from the same base in another language; (ii) transferring a fine-tune to a re-trained base needs permutation-based "re-basin" of the task vector, with special handling of attention heads and residual connections (ICML 2025). **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Huang et al. 2023/2024, Chat Vector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04799); [Rinaldi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22697)

#### What merging keeps and what it loses
- Weight averaging of fine-tuned models keeps what the models share and forgets what only one of them has: "shared knowledge among models is enhanced during model fusion, while unshared knowledge is usually forgotten" (EMNLP 2024). GPT-2 models fine-tuned on different article subsets: average likelihood ratio (lower = more memorised) on each model's private data 0.22 for the individual model vs. 0.65-0.66 after fusing three models, while on the shared subset it stays 0.22 -> 0.24; fusing four models forgets more (0.755-0.758). Validation perplexity: individual 35.25-35.81, fused 30.63, trained on all data 27.45. **[PT-small] (full)** — [Zaman, Choshen, Srivastava 2023/2024, "Fuse to Forget"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07682)

### Inferences
- The three "treatment" vectors in the present system (fine-tuned minus control, both 400 steps from the same base) are not "tasks with separate input regions", which is what weight-disentanglement theory describes. They are three changes to the training recipe on the same input distribution. The applicable theory is therefore the gradient view: tau_i is approximately the accumulated gradient contribution of ingredient i, and theta_control + tau_1 + tau_2 + tau_3 approximates a 400-step fine-tune that uses all three ingredients at once, up to second-order terms and SGD noise. Since a 400-step joint fine-tune is cheap here, task arithmetic has no cost advantage, and the literature gives no reason to expect it to beat the joint fine-tune (in the published comparisons the merged model is below the multi-task model: 0.912 vs. 0.994 normalised in Ilharco et al.).
- Favourable signs for linear composition in this setting: the fine-tunes fork from a checkpoint that is far beyond the early "stability" point (Frankle et al.: 1.5-20% of training), they are short (400 steps is inside the 128-512 step range where merged accuracy peaked for CLIP and T5), and they use the same data mix. Unfavourable signs: the base is a small model with modest "pre-training" (disentanglement and mergeability fall with model size and base quality), and at random initialisation the property is absent altogether.
- The individual treatment effects reported for this system (+2.3, +2.7 and +1 tasks, each "inconclusive") mean the vectors themselves have a low signal-to-noise ratio: each tau_i = (treatment effect) + (SGD noise of the fine-tune) - (SGD noise of the control). Summing three vectors adds three treatment effects but six noise terms. If the paired runs do not share data order and seed, noise may dominate the sum.
- Vectors computed from different base checkpoints cannot be averaged or cross-applied unless the bases belong to the same training run: every positive result above requires a shared starting point.
- The "paraphrase" vector was measured against a control that degraded (it "mostly prevented a loss from repeating the same wording"). Added to a model that never went through the repeated-wording fine-tune, it may have nothing to prevent; the gain is defined relative to that control.
- The "teacher-written tasks" vector mostly encodes memorised items; memorised, model-specific content is exactly what merging tends to lose (Horoi et al.; Zaman et al.), so its contribution to a sum is expected to be near zero.

### Gaps
- No published experiment was found that tests task arithmetic (addition, negation or analogy) on transformers of about 10M parameters trained from scratch, on program synthesis, or on any small sequence-to-sequence model without large-scale pre-training. The closest negative evidence is the random-initialisation result of Ortiz-Jimenez et al. (vision transformers, each task trained from the same random weights); the closest positive evidence is T5-small analogies and GPT-2 small negation (both pre-trained on large corpora).
- No paper was found that studies task vectors defined as "treatment minus control" (two fine-tunes that differ in one training ingredient). CaPE (Section 6) is the nearest precedent.
- Whether weight disentanglement appears after modest, narrow-domain training of a small model (the "base coder" situation) is untested; Hazimeh et al. state that the influence of pre-training factors is not yet understood.
- I could not read the figures of Zhou et al. (2025) with the exact one-epoch vs. converged merging accuracies, nor the cosine-similarity values between task vectors in Ilharco et al. (the text gives only "close to orthogonal").

## 2. Merging methods and their requirements; merging models trained from different initialisations; how close merged models get to the ensemble, and what fails

### Takeaway
There are two regimes. (A) Models fine-tuned from one shared checkpoint can be merged without any alignment: a greedy "soup" gets about 80% of the logit-ensemble's gain for one task (81.03 vs. 81.19 vs. best single 80.38), and the refinements (Fisher, RegMean, TIES, DARE, AdaMerging and others) mainly matter when the models were trained on different tasks, where the merged model still stays roughly 3-15 points under joint multi-task training. (B) Models trained from different random initialisations collapse to chance when averaged; permutation, optimal-transport and learned alignments reduce the barrier (down to about zero for small transformers with the 2025 learned alignments), but a zero barrier only means "as good as the parents": after fine-tuning, fused models recover roughly 20-40% of the ensemble gain (my arithmetic), and only methods that pull a population together during training recover more (36-86%). No method merges models of different architectures in weight space.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Models that share a starting checkpoint
- Model soups (Wortsman et al. 2022). CLIP ViT-B/32, 72 fine-tuned models, ImageNet / average of five distribution shifts: best individual 80.38 / 47.83; uniform soup 79.97 / 51.45; greedy soup 81.03 / 50.75; logit ensemble 81.19 / 50.77; greedy ensemble 81.90 / 49.44. The greedy soup "selects 5 models". ViT-G/14: best single on held-out validation 90.72, greedy soup 90.94, greedy ensemble 90.93. Greedy soup recipe: sort models by held-out validation accuracy, add each one only if the validation accuracy of the average does not drop, so it "can be no worse than the best individual model on the held-out validation set". **[PT-large] (full)** — [Wortsman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)
- Soups on text classification are smaller: greedy soup vs. best individual of 32 fine-tunes, BERT: MRPC 88.3 vs. 88.3, RTE 61.7 vs. 61.0, CoLA 59.1 vs. 59.1, SST-2 93.0 vs. 92.5; T5: MRPC 92.4 vs. 91.8, RTE 79.1 vs. 78.3, CoLA 60.2 vs. 58.8, SST-2 94.7 vs. 94.6; "the improvements are not as pronounced as in image classification". With a weaker pre-training set (ImageNet-22k instead of CLIP/ALIGN data) the soup gain is "less substantial". **[PT-small / PT-large] (full)** — [Wortsman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)
- When soups fail or lag: "there can be an error barrier between fine-tuned models", mainly "when fine-tuning with high learning rates"; "for high learning rate values, ensemble performance exceeds soup performance"; "ensembles achieve higher accuracy on ImageNet while the reverse is true on the distribution shifts"; ensembles improve calibration but "model soups do not have the same effect". With five fine-tunes that differ only in random seed, "the performance of the model soup appears correlated with the performance of the ensemble". **[PT-large] (full)** — [Wortsman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)
- Soup vs. distilling the ensemble of the same fine-tunes (Appendix L, learning rate 3e-5, ImageNet / shifts): soup 80.24 / 47.97; ensemble 80.19 / 46.33; student distilled from the ensemble 79.79 / 45.63; distilling costs about as much as training the ensemble members. **[PT-large] (full)** — [Wortsman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)
- WiSE-FT (interpolating the weights of the original and the fine-tuned model): +4 to 6 points under distribution shift and +1.6 points on ImageNet relative to prior work, 2-23 points on six further shifts, 0.8-3.3 points on seven transfer datasets, at no extra cost (CVPR 2022). **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Wortsman et al. 2021/2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01903)
- Fisher merging: per-parameter average weighted by each model's diagonal Fisher information (estimated on up to 4,096 examples for GLUE). Merging five BERT-base checkpoints "significantly outperforms isotropic merging in all cases" and "attains comparable performance to prediction ensembling"; roughly 6x cheaper than fine-tuning. Limits: "might be less performant when applied to models whose parameters are far apart in parameter space"; restricted to models "trained from the same initialization"; merging was worse than sequential training when MNLI was the donor task (RTE 73.2 merged vs. 77.4 sequential; no-donor baseline 63.7). **[PT-small] (full)** — [Matena and Raffel 2021/2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09832)
- RegMean: closed-form merge of each linear layer, W_M = (sum_i X_i^T X_i)^(-1) sum_i (X_i^T X_i W_i), needing only the Gram matrices of layer inputs. RoBERTa-base on GLUE pairs (average over 8 tasks): individual 71.76, simple average 74.22, Fisher 75.25, RegMean 75.27. Merging five emotion-classification models (RoBERTa-base, in-domain): simple 21.31, Fisher 28.27, ensemble 27.21, RegMean 38.73, multi-task training 47.75; T5-base: simple 39.52, Fisher 39.28, ensemble 38.89, RegMean 40.32, multi-task 49.06. Notes: "model merging is more successful when individual models share the same weight initialization"; in this shared-checkpoint setting no weight permutations were needed, and activation-based permutation matching gave results "far below simple average without matching"; mergeability depends on the base model (T5-base merges well, DeBERTa-large is hard to merge). **[PT-small / PT-large] (full)** — [Jin et al. 2022/2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09849)
- TIES-Merging: keep the top 20% of each task vector by magnitude, elect a sign per parameter, average only the agreeing values. Average performance (with a validation set): T5-base Task Arithmetic 73.2, TIES 73.9, multi-task 83.6; T5-large 73.3 / 76.9 / 88.1; ViT-B/32 70.1 / 73.6 / 88.9; ViT-L/14 84.5 / 86.0 / 93.5; simple averaging 65.9 (T5-base), 65.8 (ViT-B/32). "Keeping only the top-20% of values delivers comparable results to retaining all parameters"; with oracle signs from the multi-task model (IA)^3 reaches 72.0 vs. 73.1 multi-task and 66.4 TIES; merging "still lags behind the simultaneous multitask training"; "merging relies on a common initialization and model architecture" (NeurIPS 2023). **[PT-large] (full)** — [Yadav et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01708)
- DARE: randomly drop a fraction p of the delta (fine-tuned minus base) parameters and rescale the rest by 1/(1-p); "can remove 90% or even 99% delta parameters without significantly affecting the model performance". Condition: works "when the absolute values of SFT delta parameters are relatively small (e.g., within 0.002)", "otherwise, DARE may fail"; after continued pre-training "the delta parameters can rapidly reach around 0.03, making DARE infeasible". On encoder models and GLUE, adding DARE changed five merging methods by +0.58, +0.36, +0.37, -0.03 and +0.84 points on average. **[PT-large] (full)** — [Yu et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03099)
- Model Breadcrumbs: task vectors are sparsified to remove both weight outliers and negligible perturbations; "does not require hyperparameter tuning for each new task added" (ECCV 2024). **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Davari and Belilovsky 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06795)
- AdaMerging: learns per-task or per-layer merging coefficients by minimising prediction entropy on unlabelled test inputs. Eight-task average accuracy, ViT-B/32: individual 90.5, multi-task 88.9, weight averaging 65.8, Fisher 68.3, RegMean 71.8, Task Arithmetic 69.1, TIES 72.4, task-wise AdaMerging 71.1, layer-wise AdaMerging 80.1, AdaMerging++ 81.1; ViT-L/14: Task Arithmetic 84.5, TIES 86.0, AdaMerging 90.8, AdaMerging++ 91.0, multi-task 93.5. Spearman correlation between entropy and loss 0.87. **[PT-large] (full)** — [Yang et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02575)
- Model Stock: approximates the centre of the fine-tuned weight distribution from only two fine-tuned models plus the pre-trained anchor, by layer-wise averaging; reported to surpass Model Soup with "barely" any extra cost (ECCV 2024 oral). Numbers not read. **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Jang, Yun, Han 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19522)
- Evolutionary merging: evolutionary search over merge recipes in parameter space and in "data flow space" (which layers of which model to run), used to combine a Japanese LLM with a maths LLM (Nature Machine Intelligence 2025). **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Akiba et al. 2024/2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13187)
- Arithmetic on adapters: addition and negation operators for parameter-efficient modules (LoRA and similar), used for distribution generalisation, multi-tasking, unlearning, domain transfer and detoxifying an instruction-tuned LLaMA (NeurIPS 2023). LoraHub composes several LoRA modules for a new task from a few examples without gradients; it does not surpass in-context learning on Big-Bench Hard but uses fewer tokens (COLM 2024). For composing two skills, concatenating LoRAs with learned weights ("CAT") beat existing merging methods by 43% and data mixing by 12% on average on maths-with-code problems (COLING 2025 industry track). **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14870); [Huang et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13269); [Prabhakar et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13025)
- Why averaging can match ensembling: a bias-variance-covariance-locality decomposition shows weight averaging succeeds when the "variance term dominates", and diversity between averaged runs helps. **[theory + PT-large] (abs)** — [Rame et al. 2022, DiWA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09739)
- Related positive results under a shared pre-trained start: weights fine-tuned on different rewards remain "linearly connected" (rewarded soups); nodes that fine-tune independently and are averaged once at the end match communication-heavy training for DeiT and for OPT up to 1.3B (lo-fi; fine-tuning only). **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Rame et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04488); [Wortsman et al. 2022, lo-fi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11948)
- Surveys and benchmarks for orientation: a survey with a new taxonomy covering LLMs, multimodal models and more than ten sub-fields (v5, December 2025); a 2026 survey organised as Foundations, Unification strategies, Scenarios, Ecosystem; MergeBench (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks). **(abs)** — [Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07666); [Song and Zheng 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09938); [He et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10833)

#### B. Models trained from different random initialisations
- Baseline fact: interpolating two ImageNet models trained from scratch with the same hyper-parameters but different data order gives "no better than random accuracy" (as summarised in the soups paper); the stability results of Frankle et al. are in Section 1. **[scratch] (full)** — [Wortsman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482); [Frankle et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05671)
- Conjecture: once the permutation symmetry of hidden units is taken into account, SGD solutions likely have no barrier on the linear path between them. **[scratch] (abs)** — [Entezari et al. 2021/2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06296)
- Git Re-Basin: three ways to find the permutation (activation matching, needs one pass over data; weight matching, data-free, seconds to minutes; straight-through estimator, expensive). Zero-barrier connectivity on MNIST MLPs and, for the first time, between independently trained (wide) ResNets on CIFAR-10; ImageNet ResNet-50 "fall[s] short of zero-barrier connections". Stated failures: "linear mode connectivity between thin models remains elusive" (1x width fails); connectivity "is an emergent property of training, and we were unable to uncover it early in training"; models in the initial stages of training; some optimiser settings; architectures with few permutation symmetries. LayerNorm "can be merged without issue", BatchNorm statistics must be recomputed. Two ResNet-20s trained on disjoint, biased halves of CIFAR-100: the merged model "outperforms both input models in terms of test loss" at half the compute of an ensemble but "did not match the performance" of the ensemble or of training on all data (exact accuracies are only in figures). No transformer experiments. **[scratch] (full)** — [Ainsworth, Hayase, Srinivasa 2022/2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04836)
- REPAIR: aligned-then-averaged networks suffer "a collapse in the variance of their activations"; rescaling hidden units with about 5,000 examples repairs much of it. ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10: midpoint 66.0% -> 94.1% with REPAIR vs. 95.5% for the parents. ImageNet midpoints: below 1% without REPAIR, 41.1% (ResNet-18), 56.5% (ResNet-50), 64.2% (double-width ResNet-50) with it; "the barriers for these architectures on ImageNet are still relatively high"; "the size of the barrier decreases as network width is increased"; LayerNorm networks have a relatively low barrier even before REPAIR (8x width: 90.3% -> 93.2%). **[scratch] (full)** — [Jordan et al. 2022/2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08403)
- ZipIt! (merging models trained on different class subsets, allowing features to be merged within a model as well as across): joint accuracy, CIFAR-10 (5+5 classes, ResNet-20 x4 width): weight averaging 43.0, Git Re-Basin 46.2, permute 58.4, ZipIt! 79.1, ZipIt! with the last layers left unmerged (13/20) 83.8, ensemble 87.4. CIFAR-100 (50+50, x8 width): 17.0 / 40.9 / 42.8 / 54.9 / 70.2 / ensemble 73.5. ImageNet (200+200, ResNet-50): full merge 8.6, merging only the first 10 of 50 layers 60.9, ensemble 63.3. "When models are too small", ZipIt! and plain permutation "perform identically". **[scratch (that the models were trained from scratch is from memory)] (full)** — [Stoica et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03053)
- Optimal-transport fusion (soft neuron alignment, then averaging). One-shot: VGG-11 on CIFAR-10, parents 90.31 / 90.50, ensemble 91.34, plain averaging 17.02, OT 85.98; ResNet-18, parents 93.11 / 93.20, ensemble 93.89, plain 18.49, OT 77.00; MNIST MLP, parents 97.72 / 97.75, ensemble 97.88, plain 73.84, OT 96.54. After fine-tuning the fused model: VGG-11 90.73 (best parent 90.50, ensemble 91.34); ResNet-18 93.78 (retrained individual 93.51, ensemble 93.89); four VGG-11 on CIFAR-100: parents 62.50-62.92, ensemble 66.32, fused and fine-tuned 64.29. When one parent has a special skill (only it saw digit 4), one-shot OT fusion beats both parents (93.11 vs. 89.78 and 87.35) (NeurIPS 2020). **[scratch-small / scratch] (full)** — [Singh and Jaggi 2019/2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05653)
- Transformer fusion with optimal transport (vision transformers with 7 layers and width 384, and a 6-layer BERT, all trained from scratch with different seeds). One-shot, CIFAR-10, two models: parents 92.34 / 92.31, plain averaging 7.59, OT 57.23-60.87. One-shot, CIFAR-100: parents about 64-65, plain 0.77, OT 11.70-13.32. One-shot, Tiny ImageNet: plain 0.44, OT 1.64-3.03 (parents 43.78-45.30, ensemble of five 51.28). After fine-tuning: CIFAR-100 two models, parents 64.94 / 64.66, ensemble 68.04, fused 65.80 (+0.86); six models, ensemble 70.71, fused 65.98 (+1.04); ImageNet-1k two models, parents 75.33 / 74.88, ensemble 76.56, fused 75.80 (+0.47), while plain averaging followed by fine-tuning reaches only 67.83. BERT masked-LM loss: parents 1.612-1.807, fused and fine-tuned 2.224, plain averaging and fine-tuned 2.946. Soft (Sinkhorn) alignment "consistently outperforms" hard permutations. The paper claims to be "an effective and efficient alternative to knowledge distillation" but runs no distillation baseline (ICLR 2024). **[scratch-small / scratch] (full)** — [Imfeld et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05719)
- Merging text transformers from different initialisations (five BERT-base models from different seeds): permuting attention heads and feed-forward units lowers the masked-LM loss barrier from 4.31 +/- 0.21 (plain averaging) to 3.71 +/- 0.23; for the residual stream "an identity permutation performs significantly better than all other proposed approaches"; on eight GLUE fine-tunes the barrier fell on 6 of 8 tasks; the authors did not find a linear low-loss path and say the minima are "connected with a barrier at least as high as what we report". **[scratch, BERT-base size: five independently pre-trained seeds] (full)** — [Verma and Elbayad 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00986)
- Generalised linear mode connectivity for transformers (NeurIPS 2025 oral): alignments beyond permutations (semi-permutations, orthogonal maps, general invertible maps). Loss barriers between independently trained small models: ViT (6 layers, width 256) on CIFAR-10: plain averaging 1.69, activation matching 1.27, learned permutations 0.45, their weight matching 0.36, their learned matching 0.00; CIFAR-100: 2.46 -> 0.00; Tiny ImageNet (8 layers, width 384): 2.84 -> 0.00; GPT-2-style model (6 layers, width 256) on Tiny Shakespeare: 2.02 -> 0.02; GPT-2-style (6 layers, width 512) on BookCorpus: 4.34 -> 0.42. The learned matching is optimised with the task loss on data; a non-zero barrier remains on the larger language task; no comparison with ensembles. **[scratch-small] (full)** — [Theus et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22712)
- CCA Merge aligns with linear combinations of features instead of one-to-one permutations and is reported to work "significantly better than past methods" when more than two models are merged (ICML 2024). Numbers not read. **[scratch] (abs)** — [Horoi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05385)
- Making a population mergeable during training instead of afterwards. PAPA: networks trained from scratch with different seeds, data orders and augmentations, each pushed slightly towards the population average every 10 steps (rate 0.99); final accuracy of best single baseline / baseline ensemble / PAPA soup: CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 96.8 / 97.5 / 97.4; CIFAR-100 VGG-16 77.8 / 82.2 / 79.4; CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 80.2 / 84.3 / 82.1; ImageNet ResNet-50 76.8 / 78.7 / 78.4. Averaging independently trained baselines collapses (10.00% and 1.00% for ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, 0.97% for VGG-16 on CIFAR-100), and the authors found "no benefits of permutation alignment before averaging"; "ensembles perform better than PAPA"; "the benefit of PAPA is significant for pre-training but small for fine-tuning". **[scratch] (full)** — [Jolicoeur-Martineau et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03094)
- Other training-time variants: WASH keeps a population in one basin by "randomly shuffling a small percentage of weights during training"; Lookaround trains several copies from a common point with different augmentations and averages them repeatedly (NeurIPS 2023); SWAP refines a large-batch solution with several independent workers and averages them. Numbers not read. **[scratch] (abs)** — [Fournier et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17517); [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07684); [Gupta et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02312)
- Directly training the average of several differently initialised networks gives performance "much worse than ensemble" and "is unable to improve the test accuracy even comparing to single models". **[scratch] (full text, numbers in figures not read)** — [Allen-Zhu and Li 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09816)

- Shared checkpoint, same task: greedy soup recovers (81.03 - 80.38) / (81.19 - 80.38) = 80% of the ensemble's gain over the best single model; the uniform soup of all 72 is below the best single model in-distribution. — [Wortsman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)
- Shared checkpoint, different tasks: merged models reach 74-86 against 84-94 for multi-task training (TIES with a validation set) and 80-91 with test-time coefficient learning (AdaMerging). — [Yadav et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01708); [Yang et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02575)
- Different initialisations, aligned and fine-tuned: 28% (two ViTs, CIFAR-100) and 18% (six ViTs) of the ensemble gain; 38% on ImageNet-1k; 40% for four VGG-11 on CIFAR-100. — [Imfeld et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05719); [Singh and Jaggi 2019/2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05653)
- Different initialisations, pulled together during training (PAPA): 36% (CIFAR-100 VGG-16), 46% (CIFAR-100 ResNet-18), 86% (CIFAR-10 ResNet-18), 84% (ImageNet ResNet-50). — [Jolicoeur-Martineau et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03094)
- Different tasks and different initialisations (ZipIt!): 79.1 vs. 87.4 for the ensemble on CIFAR-10 halves, 54.9 vs. 73.5 on CIFAR-100 halves, 8.6 vs. 63.3 on ImageNet unless most layers are left unmerged. — [Stoica et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03053)

### Inferences
- The sibling fine-tunes of this system (same base, 400 steps) are in regime A: plain averaging should work without alignment, and applying permutation matching there is unnecessary and can hurt (RegMean authors' observation). The roughly twenty committee members (different seeds, data mixes, two decoder orders) are in regime B.
- For regime B at this scale the evidence is consistent: plain averaging of independently trained small transformers gives chance-level models (7.59% on CIFAR-10 for 7-layer ViTs; 0.97-10% for the PAPA baselines), one-shot aligned fusion is far below the parents except on the easiest task, and fine-tuning the fused model ends up 0.2-1.4 points above a parent while the ensemble is 0.7-5.8 points above. The 2025 learned alignments reach zero barrier for small transformers, but a zero barrier means the midpoint is as good as the end points, not better: there is no published case where post-hoc alignment of independently trained models reaches ensemble accuracy.
- Coders with prefix-order and postfix-order decoders cannot be merged with each other by any of these methods: all of them assume the same architecture and the same function being computed by matched units.
- DARE's precondition (small deltas, around 0.002 in absolute value for LLMs) should be checked before using drop-and-rescale on short high-learning-rate fine-tunes of a small model; the equivalent check is the ratio of task-vector norm to weight norm per layer.
- AdaMerging's entropy objective is defined for classification outputs; for a program generator an analogous unsupervised objective would have to be invented (for example agreement with a committee), which is untested.
- RegMean and Fisher merging need only per-layer input statistics or squared gradients from a few thousand examples; at 12.4M parameters these cost seconds, so they are cheap variants to include whenever sibling fine-tunes are merged.

### Gaps
- Exact accuracies of the Git Re-Basin split-data experiment (merged vs. ensemble) are only in figures and were not read. Numbers for Model Stock, Model Breadcrumbs, CCA Merge, WASH, Lookaround and SWAP were not read (abstracts only).
- No study was found that merges small sequence-to-sequence or program-synthesis transformers trained from scratch, or that compares post-hoc merging with a behavioural-agreement committee rather than a logit ensemble.
- No evidence was found on LoRA arithmetic or LoraHub-style composition for models around 10M parameters trained from scratch; all adapter-composition results are for pre-trained LLMs.
- Theus et al. give no ensemble comparison and only a qualitative three-model result; whether learned alignment plus fine-tuning can beat a parent for small language models is open.

## 3. Weight averaging within and across runs (SWA, EMA, LAWA, Lookahead, snapshot ensembles), and whether averaging delays or reduces overfitting when data are small

### Takeaway
Averaging weights along one training run reliably gives a better model than the raw iterate while the learning rate is still high, costs almost nothing, and behaves like an ensemble of nearby checkpoints; but its gain at the end of a fully annealed run is small (for a 124M language model, validation loss 3.0425 vs. 3.0421 and 3.0413) and it does not replace learning-rate decay. For small-data overfitting the evidence is thin but points one way: averaging raises and steadies the peak (a 5M-parameter network trained on about 1,000 puzzles: 79.9% -> 87.4% with EMA; under 40% label noise an early-stopped EMA gains 8.6-9.7 points), yet the averaged model memorises too once the trajectory does, so the averaging window has to be placed around the validation optimum and early stopping is still needed.

### Cited Findings

#### Classic within-run averaging
- SWA (average checkpoints collected with a cyclical or constant learning rate after about 75% of the budget). Test accuracy, SGD -> SWA at the same budget (and at 1.5x budget), with the FGE ensemble for comparison: CIFAR-100 VGG-16 72.55 -> 73.91 (74.27), FGE 74.26; Preactivation ResNet-164 78.49 -> 79.77 (80.35), FGE 79.84; Wide ResNet-28-10 80.82 -> 81.46 (82.15), FGE 82.27. CIFAR-10 Wide ResNet-28-10 96.18 -> 96.45 (96.79). ImageNet with 10 extra SWA epochs: ResNet-50 76.15 -> 76.97, ResNet-152 78.31 -> 78.94, DenseNet-161 77.65 -> 78.44. The paper summarises "consistent improvement by about 0.5%" on CIFAR-10 and "0.75-1.5%" on CIFAR-100. **[scratch] (full)** — [Izmailov et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05407)
- SWA as a one-model ensemble: "SWA can be interpreted as an approximation to FGE ensembles"; label agreement between the SWA model and the FGE ensemble is 95.26%. "The training loss for SWA is often slightly worse than for SGD" while test error is better; SWA ends up centred in a flat region where SGD sits near its boundary. Training from scratch at a fixed learning rate (Wide ResNet-28-10, CIFAR-100, averaging from epoch 140 of 300) gave 81.7%: "SGD with a constant learning rate oscillates around the optimum, but SWA converges". **[scratch] (full)** — [Izmailov et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05407)
- A later re-examination finds that the averaging step reduces variance but "does not always lead to wide optima", and proposes periodic SWA. **[scratch] (abs)** — [Guo, Jin, Liu 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00519)
- Snapshot ensembles: one run with a cyclic learning rate visits several minima; saving and ensembling the snapshots costs no extra training; DenseNet snapshot ensembles reach 3.4% error on CIFAR-10 and 17.4% on CIFAR-100. **[scratch] (abs)** — [Huang et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00109)
- Lookahead: keeps "fast" and "slow" weights and is reported to improve learning stability and lower the variance of the inner optimiser at negligible cost, on ImageNet, CIFAR-10/100, translation and Penn Treebank (NeurIPS 2019). **[scratch] (abs)** — [Zhang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08610)

#### Averaging the latest checkpoints (LAWA) and EMA: large gains mid-training, small gains at the end
- LAWA: average the k most recent end-of-epoch checkpoints, default k = 6; "averaging too many checkpoints (k>16) results in worse performance"; averaging during the early, fluctuating phase is worse than the baseline. ResNet-50 on ImageNet reaches about 66% validation accuracy about 40 epochs (about 68 GPU-hours) earlier; RoBERTa-base on WikiText-103 reaches the baseline's final best validation loss about 45 epochs (about 30 GPU-hours) earlier. But the head start "decreases towards the end of the training, and the highest reached accuracy is not reached much earlier". **[scratch] (full)** — [Kaddour 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14981)
- LAWA with high learning rates for language-model pre-training (nanoGPT-2 on OpenWebText; Pythia 1B-12B). Validation loss at 70K steps, original / EMA / SWA / LAWA: GPT-2 small (125M) 2.963 / 2.949 / 2.952 / 2.917; medium (355M) 2.855 / 2.845 / 2.837 / 2.819; large (770M) 2.977 / 2.968 / 2.961 / 2.908. "We observe higher gains in the early-mid stages of pre-training, and the gains start diminishing towards the final stages"; models trained with higher learning rates gain more; checkpoints spaced further apart gain more; "averaging very initially i.e. before 8K steps during training may not always yield beneficial results"; "applying SWA during the early stages of training leads to divergence". **[scratch] (full)** — [Sanyal et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03241)
- Benchmark study on seven AlgoPerf workloads plus a 124M language model: LAWA and EMA reach the target score in about 82% of the baseline's steps (language model: about 2,200-2,240 steps instead of 3,200); final generalisation gains are "minimal" (WMT BLEU 31.157 -> 31.429 / 31.457; language-model validation loss 3.0425 -> 3.0421 / 3.0413); the early advantage "shrinks towards the end of training"; averaging "cannot fully replace a learning rate annealing", but "combining the two yields slightly better final validation scores"; "an averaging window around 1% of the total training budget consistently yields optimal results", and a long horizon "may prioritize outdated model checkpoints and generalize worse". On the one over-fitting workload, averaged models were better throughout "but eventually regress to the overfitting baseline algorithm". **[scratch] (full)** — [Ajroldi, Orvieto, Geiping 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06761)
- For 33M-360M language models: "we find a significant performance boost for SWA on top of a constant LR. Yet, it does not reach the loss values of explicit cooldowns"; windows "below or at 2500 steps" were best; EMA "performed worse than SWA". **[scratch] (full)** — [Hägele et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18392)
- Checkpoint averaging during pre-training of 411M-200B models ("PMA"): averaging checkpoints from the constant-learning-rate phase gives large gains and "can effectively match the performance of an annealed model at any point in the training process", while it "yields diminished improvements when models are annealed to a very low learning rate"; ten checkpoints were used; differences between simple, weighted and exponential averages "gradually become negligible"; windows that include "unstable weights from the initial training phase" under-perform; restarting from an averaged checkpoint recovers unstable runs (NeurIPS 2025). **[scratch, large] (full)** — [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12082)
- In late pre-training, consecutive averaged checkpoints "collapse onto a stable, approximately one-dimensional linear manifold", and averaging acts as a low-pass filter on high-curvature noise; extrapolating along that line ("Extra-Merge") lowers loss further for GPT-2 and LLaMA models from 124M to 2B (ICML 2026 according to the proceedings listing). **[scratch] (abs; the 124M GPT-2 small on FineWeb set-up and the "over 94% of variance" figure are snippet-level)** — [Zhou et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26484)
- In neural machine translation, checkpoint averaging is needed for the best result, but the region between converged checkpoints is "rather flat", so more elaborate selection or weighting schemes add little (AACL 2022). **[scratch] (abs)** — [Gao et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11803)

#### Averaging and overfitting when data are small or noisy
- A tiny network trained on about 1,000 examples (Tiny Recursive Model: 5M parameters on Sudoku-Extreme, 7M on ARC): "On small data ... [the predecessor] tends to overfit quickly and then diverge"; an EMA of the weights with decay 0.999 "prevents sharp collapse and leads to higher generalization (going from 79.9% to 87.4%" test accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme). **[scratch-small] (full)** — [Jolicoeur-Martineau 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871)
- Systematic EMA study (ResNet-18, Wide ResNet-28-10, VGG-16; CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet; TMLR 2024). Clean data, SGD vs. best EMA: CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 77.63 -> 78.55, Wide ResNet-28-10 81.07 -> 82.72; Tiny-ImageNet 66.03 -> 67.97. With about 40% human label noise (ResNet-34): CIFAR-100N 55.47 -> 65.15, CIFAR-10N 78.09 -> 86.71, where the EMA model is picked at its validation peak ("the EMA model peaks at 65.15% accuracy at epoch 100, with a learning rate around 0.4"). The EMA does memorise eventually: "Memorization in the EMA occurs as the learning rate is decayed, and not due to continued training"; when the EMA reaches 90% accuracy on the clean training labels (epoch 99) it has memorised 28.8% of the noisy labels, whereas the paper's SGD comparison point (epoch 142) has 68.2%. With EMA plus early stopping "the entire last phase of training can be spared"; solutions reached by averaging "are different than by learning rate decay" (lower prediction churn 18.84 -> 11.69, better calibration). Limitation: image classification only. **[scratch] (full)** — [Morales-Brotons, Vogels, Hendrikx 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18704)
- SWAD (domain generalisation from an ImageNet-pre-trained ResNet-50): with small data "a simple ERM approach is rapidly reached to a local optimum only within a few epochs", so "directly applying the vanilla SWA will suffer from the overfitting issue by averaging sub-optimal solutions". Fix: average every iteration ("dense") inside an "overfit-aware" window that starts when validation loss stops decreasing for N_s = 3 evaluations and ends when it exceeds r = 1.2-1.3 times the loss at the start for N_e = 6 evaluations. Results: 66.9% vs. 63.3% average out-of-domain accuracy for plain training (+3.6 points; +1.6 over the best previous method), ImageNet in-domain 76.5 -> 77.0. **[PT-large] (full)** — [Cha et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08604)
- Theory: for least squares, geometrically weighted iterate averaging of SGD has a regularising effect and is asymptotically equivalent to ridge regression. **[theory] (abs)** — [Neu and Rosasco 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08009)
- Speech recognition practice: choose the stopping point and the checkpoints to average by an approximate bias-variance trade-off (training loss as proxy for bias, validation loss for variance) instead of the usual recipe; reported character-error-rate reductions of 2.5%-3.7% and 3.1%-4.6% on two Mandarin corpora. **[scratch] (abs)** — [Wang et al. 2023, ApproBiVT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02870)

#### Averaging across runs
- Independently trained networks cannot be averaged after the fact, but populations pulled together during training (PAPA, WASH, Lookaround) can; numbers are in Section 2B. **[scratch] (full for PAPA)** — [Jolicoeur-Martineau et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03094)
- Averaging models that were fine-tuned from one checkpoint on different data subsets forgets what only one model memorised and keeps what they share (numbers in Section 1). **[PT-small] (full)** — [Zaman, Choshen, Srivastava 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07682)

### Inferences
- What averaging can add at the 30-minute peak depends on the learning rate there. Every source agrees that the gain is large while the learning rate is high and nearly vanishes once it has been annealed. If the coders are trained with a schedule that is still high at 30 minutes, an averaged checkpoint should beat the raw one; if the schedule is already low, expect almost nothing.
- Averaging along one run cannot remove what all checkpoints in the window have already memorised, and a running EMA gradually forgets the pre-memorisation weights as training continues. "Holding the peak" with averaging therefore means freezing the average over a window around the validation optimum. It is early stopping with a smoother, less noisy model, not a cure for the later decline (the AlgoPerf over-fitting case and the label-noise EMA result both show the averaged model following the trajectory into over-fitting).
- A side benefit that matters with a 139-task evaluation set: an averaged checkpoint is less sensitive to which exact step is picked, so it reduces the "lucky checkpoint" effect in model selection.
- The two results closest to this setting (tiny model, about 1,000 examples, +7.5 points from EMA; and 40% label noise, +8.6 to +9.7 points) suggest the upside can be larger for small noisy training sets than the 0.5-1.5 points seen on clean benchmarks, but both are single studies outside language or program generation.
- Transformers with LayerNorm need no recomputation of normalisation statistics after averaging (the BatchNorm caveat in SWA, REPAIR and the EMA study does not apply).
- A different, across-run use of averaging follows from "Fuse to Forget": fork one base checkpoint, continue each fork on a different part of the small natural-language data (plus the shared synthetic data), then average. The literature predicts that shared skills survive and fork-specific memorisation is diluted. This is untested for small from-scratch models and each fork sees fewer distinct examples, so it could also memorise faster; it is a cheap experiment rather than an expectation.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures memorisation of a small natural-language subset in a small from-scratch transformer with and without weight averaging; the over-fitting evidence comes from puzzles (TRM), image classification with label noise, domain generalisation and speech.
- The claim that weight averaging remains prone to "robust overfitting" in adversarial training appeared only in a search snippet ([Rebuffi et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05328)) and was not verified.
- "No Train No Gain" (Kaddour et al. 2023) does not evaluate weight averaging; it only remarks in related work that such "gains arise primarily through effectively lowering the learning rates" ([Kaddour et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06440)). I had expected a direct test there and did not find one.
- Exact numbers for SWAP, Lookaround, WASH and Extra-Merge were not read.

## 4. Compressing an ensemble into one model (distillation from ensembles, born-again networks, co-distillation, BabyLlama and the BabyLM challenge, the multi-view explanation), and how much of the ensemble's gain a same-size student keeps, especially for sequence generation

### Takeaway
A same-size student usually keeps a large part, but not all, of an ensemble's gain: 86% in Hinton's speech model, 58-83% in machine translation (most of which a single teacher already gives), about 42% on ImageNet, while its agreement with the ensemble stays low and the benefit saturates after two to five teachers. For small language models trained from scratch on small data the result is stronger: the distilled student beats each teacher and the best of 265 undistilled runs (BabyLlama-2). For sequence generation the practical form is sequence-level distillation, that is, training on the outputs the committee selects for many unlabelled inputs. How much is kept depends strongly on how many unlabelled inputs are used (15% of the gain with 30k sentences, more than 100% with 200k in one study), and training on the model's own ordinary outputs gives nothing or hurts.

### Cited Findings

#### Classic results and theory
- Speech recognition: baseline 58.9% frame accuracy / 10.9% word error rate; ensemble of 10 models 61.1% / 10.7%; single model distilled from the ensemble 60.8% / 10.7%: "the distilled single model performs about as well as the averaged predictions of 10 models". MNIST: large net 67 test errors, small net 146, small net trained on the large net's soft targets 74. **[scratch] (full)** — [Hinton, Vinyals, Dean 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531)
- Soft targets as protection against over-fitting on little data: with only 3% of the speech training data, hard targets give 44.5% test frame accuracy (67.3% train) and need early stopping because "the accuracy drops sharply after reaching 44.5%", whereas soft targets from the full-data model give 57.0% (65.4% train) and "simply 'converged' to 57%"; the full-data baseline is 58.9%. **[scratch] (full)** — [Hinton, Vinyals, Dean 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531)
- Multi-view explanation: when the data contain several features ("views") that each suffice for most examples, a single network learns only some of them depending on its random initialisation, an ensemble covers them all, and distilling the ensemble forces one network to learn the views it would otherwise miss; self-distillation "is performing implicit ensemble together with knowledge distillation". In the theory the test error is about 0.49-0.51 mu for a single model, at most 0.001 mu for the ensemble and for the distilled single model, and at most 0.26 mu for a self-distilled model. Stated empirical points: an ensemble of ten ResNet-34 on CIFAR-100 "can easily offer a 5% improvement"; an ensemble of models that were themselves distilled "does not exceed the ensemble over the original single models"; ensembles of random-feature (kernel-regime) models do not behave this way and "knowledge distillation does not work for random feature mappings". **[theory + scratch] (full text; figure tables not read)** — [Allen-Zhu and Li 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09816)
- Born-again networks (student with the same architecture trained on the teacher's outputs). CIFAR-100 test error, teacher -> student: DenseNet-112-33 18.25 -> 16.95, DenseNet-90-60 17.69 -> 16.69, DenseNet-80-80 17.16 -> 16.36, DenseNet-80-120 16.87 -> 16.00; Wide-ResNet-28-10 19.08 -> 18.25, but Wide-ResNet-28-5 20.88 -> 20.93. Penn Treebank perplexity (test): ConvLSTM 80.05 -> 76.97, LSTM 71.87 -> 68.56, and the language models work "only when trained with a combination of teacher outputs and label loss". Gains over further generations "saturate after a few generations". **[scratch] (full)** — [Furlanello et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04770)
- Online co-distillation (n copies trained in parallel, each also matching the average prediction of the others, possibly from stale checkpoints): two-way co-distillation of an LSTM language model on about 6x10^11 tokens "reaches the same error in 2X fewer steps"; ImageNet 75% accuracy after 5,250 instead of 7,250 steps and 75.6% at the end; click-prediction log loss 0.4480 (single), 0.4461 (ensemble of two), 0.4458 (co-distilled); prediction churn between retrains 0.029 (single), 0.022 (ensemble), 0.019 (co-distilled). Label-smoothing controls did not reproduce the gain. **[scratch] (full)** — [Anil et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.03235)
- Ensemble distribution distillation: distil "the distribution of the predictions from an ensemble, rather than just the average prediction" into one prior network, keeping the ensemble's diversity for uncertainty estimates while approaching its accuracy on CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet. **[scratch] (abs)** — [Malinin, Mlodozeniec, Gales 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00076)
- Fidelity is low even when distillation helps: "there often remains a surprisingly large discrepancy between the predictive distributions of the teacher and the student", including with identical capacity; ImageNet, teacher ensembles of 1 / 3 / 5 models reach 74.8 / 76.4 / 76.7% and their students 75.3 / 75.5 / 75.6% with test agreement 85.5 / 87.8 / 88.4%; student accuracy "does not substantially improve as we increase the number m of models in the teacher ensemble past 4"; the cause is optimisation difficulty, and "good student accuracy does not imply good distillation fidelity" (NeurIPS 2021). **[scratch] (full)** — [Stanton et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05945)
- For fine-tunes of one checkpoint, a student distilled from the ensemble (79.79) is slightly below both the ensemble (80.19) and the weight-averaged soup (80.24). **[PT-large] (full)** — [Wortsman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)
- A student built from several sub-networks that are averaged into one network after training, combined with perturbed inputs on which the teachers disagree, is reported to "significantly improve upon previous methods" of ensemble distillation (ICML 2022). **[scratch] (abs)** — [Nam et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15047)
- Distillation as regularisation: early stopping is argued to be the main reason distillation helps, and distilling each epoch from the previous epoch's network prevents memorising wrong labels; a few rounds of self-distillation reduce over-fitting but further rounds under-fit. **[theory] (abs)** — [Dong et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01255); [Mobahi, Farajtabar, Bartlett 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05715)

#### Small language models trained from scratch on small data (BabyLM)
- BabyLlama (10M-word corpus): teachers GPT-2 (705M) and a small LLaMA (360M), student a 58M LLaMA trained with 0.5 x cross-entropy + 0.5 x KL to the averaged teacher distribution at temperature 2. "The smaller, distilled model outperforms both of its teachers individually", and beats the same 58M model trained without distillation on most BLiMP categories, in several also the teacher ensemble (irregular forms 93.1 vs. 74.1 undistilled and 83.4 ensemble; subject-auxiliary inversion 88.5 vs. 77.4 and 84.5; determiner-noun agreement 90.8 vs. 87.8 and 89.8), with exceptions (island effects 51.2 vs. 57.3 undistilled). Two teachers were best among 2, 4 and 6. **[scratch] (full)** — [Timiryasov and Tastet 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02019)
- BabyLlama-2 (same-size distillation: 345M student, two 345M teachers, 10M words, weight 0.5, temperature 1). Macro-average over BLiMP, BLiMP supplement, EWoK and SuperGLUE: student 64.0 and 64.2 (two runs) vs. teachers 61.3 / 63.6 and 62.9 / 63.1, and vs. 62.9 for the best checkpoint of a 265-model hyper-parameter sweep without distillation; the advantage "cannot be attributed to suboptimal hyperparameter selection of the teachers". More than two teachers gave "diminishing returns". "The exact origin of the improvements from distillation-pretraining remains unclear"; scalability to more data is "unexplored". **[scratch] (full)** — [Tastet and Timiryasov 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17312)
- Teacher-less mutual learning among small peer models, with learned peer weights, "can match or surpass teacher-supervised approaches" on BabyLM data (BabyLM 2024). **[scratch] (abs)** — [Iyer 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16487)
- Findings of the second BabyLM challenge: 31 submissions; "a hybrid causal-masked language model architecture outperformed other approaches"; "a strong relationship between training FLOPs and average performance across tasks". The abstract does not single out distillation. **[scratch] (abs)** — [Hu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05149)

#### Sequence generation
- Sequence-level distillation for translation (student trained on the teacher's beam-search outputs). English-German: teacher (4x1000 LSTM) 17.7 greedy / 19.5 beam; student (2x500) baseline 14.7 / 17.6; with word-level distillation 15.4 / 17.7; with sequence-level distillation 18.9 / 19.0; with sequence-level distillation plus fine-tuning towards the teacher candidate closest to the reference 18.9 / 19.3. The distilled student's distribution is much more peaked: the greedy output carries 16.9% of the probability mass against 0.9% for the baseline, so greedy decoding is nearly as good as beam search; perplexity on references rises from 8.2 to 22.7. No same-size student is tested. **[scratch] (full)** — [Kim and Rush 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07947)
- Ensemble distillation for translation with a same-architecture student (German-English, BLEU / TER): single baseline 27.4 / 53.7; ensemble of six 29.8 / 51.2; student trained on the ensemble's translations plus the original data 29.1 / 51.8; with filtering of poor teacher translations 29.4 / 51.5; student of a single same-size teacher 28.8 / 52.3; a much smaller student (300 hidden units) 27.8 / 53.1. **[scratch] (full)** — [Freitag, Al-Onaizan, Sankaran 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01802)
- Distilling an expensive selection procedure (minimum Bayes risk decoding over 256 sampled candidates) into the model itself, English-German, COMET20: base model with beam search 57.79; base model with MBR decoding 59.35; student fine-tuned on its own MBR outputs for 30k sentences 58.02; student fine-tuned on its own MBR outputs for 200k unlabelled sentences 59.94; fine-tuning on its own beam outputs 57.08 ("beam finetuning ... degrades performance"; "performance improvements are not observed when finetuning on self-beam translations"); with a stronger external teacher's MBR outputs 63.51. On the metric that MBR optimised (BLEURT) the student stays far below MBR decoding (77.79 vs. 81.09), which the authors attribute to metric over-fitting of MBR. **[scratch, large translation model trained on 89M sentence pairs; the external teacher is a pre-trained LLM] (full)** — [Finkelstein et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10966)
- Two 2026 preprints on code and reasoning with large pre-trained models: fine-tuning a model on its own sampled solutions (no verifier, no teacher) raised pass@1 on LiveCodeBench v6 from 42.4% to 55.3% for a 30B model; distilling the majority answer of sampled solutions back into the model as token-level supervision improved pass@1 by up to 12 points and the model then "solves problems it never solved in 32 attempts before". **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Zhang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01193); [Gkountouras, Jukić, Titov 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13643)

#### Share of the ensemble gain kept by a same-size student (my arithmetic from the numbers above)
- Speech frame accuracy: (60.8 - 58.9) / (61.1 - 58.9) = 86%; word error rate: all of it. — [Hinton, Vinyals, Dean 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531)
- Translation: 71% (29.1) to 83% (29.4 with filtering) of the six-model ensemble's +2.4 BLEU; a single same-size teacher already gives 58%, so the part due to the ensemble itself is 0.3-0.6 BLEU. — [Freitag, Al-Onaizan, Sankaran 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01802)
- ImageNet: 42% of the five-model ensemble's gain over one teacher (75.6 vs. 74.8 and 76.7), and only +0.3 points more than a student of a single teacher. — [Stanton et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05945)
- MBR selection distilled into the model: 15% of the gain with 30k sentences, 138% (the student exceeds the teacher procedure) with 200k unlabelled sentences on COMET20. — [Finkelstein et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10966)
- Small language models on small data: the student exceeds its teachers by 0.4-1.1 macro points and the best undistilled model by 1.1-1.3; the ensemble's own score is not reported there. — [Tastet and Timiryasov 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17312)

### Inferences
- The committee result for this system (57.8 -> 72.9, most of it from the first five members) fits the multi-view picture: coders from different seeds and data mixes have learned different partial sets of features. The theory and the BabyLM results say a single same-size student can absorb a good part of that, and the published students stop improving after two to five teachers, so distilling from a five-member committee should be enough.
- A committee that selects by behavioural agreement is the program-synthesis analogue of MBR decoding, so "MBR fine-tuning" is the closest published template: label a large pool of unlabelled tasks with the committee's chosen program, train the student on them. The translation numbers say the amount of unlabelled data is decisive, and the necessary control is a student trained on a single coder's own top answers (which gave no gain or a loss there). A task generator that can produce unlimited synthetic inputs is an advantage for this route.
- Token-level soft targets (Hinton-style, BabyLlama-style) need all teachers to share the student's output order and vocabulary. Coders with the other decoder order can only contribute through sequence-level distillation (their chosen program, re-serialised in the student's order).
- Sequence-level distillation makes the student's distribution much more peaked (0.9% -> 16.9% of the mass on the greedy output in Kim and Rush). In a system that relies on a beam of distinct candidates that are then checked against the example, this can reduce candidate diversity, so the number of distinct example-fitting candidates per task should be monitored.
- Hinton's 3%-data experiment and the "distillation is like early stopping" theory suggest a second possible benefit here: soft or committee-chosen targets may slow the memorisation that makes accuracy fall after 30 minutes. In Hinton's case the teacher had seen 33 times more data, which is not true of a committee trained on the same data, so this is a hypothesis.
- An ensemble of distilled students should not be expected to beat the original committee (Allen-Zhu and Li), so distillation trades committee size for single-model quality; it does not stack indefinitely.

### Gaps
- The CIFAR accuracy tables of Allen-Zhu and Li (single vs. ensemble vs. distilled vs. self-distilled) are in figures that I could not read; only the statements quoted above are verified.
- No published result was found for distilling an ensemble or a behavioural-agreement committee into a same-size student for small program-synthesis models trained from scratch; the two 2026 code-generation self-distillation papers use large pre-trained LLMs and were read at abstract level only.
- The BabyLM 2023 and 2025 findings papers were not read; two other BabyLM 2024 distillation entries (a contrastive/adversarial variant of BabyLlama and a self-distillation/reverse-distillation entry) appeared only in search snippets and are not cited.
- I found no measurement of how sequence-level distillation changes beam diversity in program synthesis.

## 5. Continual knowledge injection without forgetting, at small scale (experience replay, EWC, Learning without Forgetting, orthogonal gradients, adapters with routing, task-vector-based continual learning, knowledge editing)

### Takeaway
Replaying old data is the most reliable remedy at every scale, and published settings need far less than a half-and-half mix: one stored example per class already beats EWC and gradient-projection methods for small networks, and 0.25-1% of old data keeps 98-99.8% of old performance for large pre-trained models. Weight-space variants (interpolating old and new weights, merging sequentially fine-tuned task vectors, staying on a linear path to the multi-task solution) help on top of replay, mostly shown for pre-trained models, and leave a large gap to joint training (74.5 vs. 87.4 for MagMax). Knowledge editing (ROME, MEMIT) changes individual facts in billion-parameter language models, breaks down after hundreds to thousands of sequential edits, and I found no evidence for it on tiny models or for rules of the kind a program writer needs.

### Cited Findings

#### Replay and its small-scale evidence
- Experience replay with a tiny memory: "a very simple baseline ... significantly outperforms specifically designed CL approaches", "with gains between 7% and 17% when the memory is populated with a single example per class". Average accuracy / forgetting with one stored example per class: Permuted MNIST: plain fine-tuning 53.5 / 0.29, EWC 63.1 / 0.18, A-GEM 62.1 / 0.21, MER 69.9 / 0.14, replay (ring buffer) 70.2 / 0.12; Split CIFAR: 40.6 / 0.27, 41.2 / 0.27, 54.9 / 0.14, 49.7 / 0.19, 56.2 / 0.13; Split miniImageNet: 34.7, 37.7, 48.2, 45.5, 49.0. "Repetitive training on even tiny memories of past tasks does not harm generalization", because training on later tasks "acts like a data dependent regularizer". Models: a two-layer MLP with 256 units and a reduced ResNet-18. **[scratch-small for MNIST/CIFAR/miniImageNet (standard practice for these benchmarks; not stated explicitly in the text I read); one further dataset uses ImageNet pre-training] (full)** — [Chaudhry et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)
- Small networks, 20 tasks, one stored example per class per task (accuracy / forgetting on Permuted MNIST, Rotated MNIST, Split CIFAR-100): naive SGD 44.4 / 0.53, 46.3 / 0.52, 40.4 / 0.31; EWC 70.7 / 0.23, 48.5 / 0.48, 42.7 / 0.28; A-GEM 65.7 / 0.29, 55.3 / 0.42, 50.7 / 0.19; replay with reservoir sampling 72.4 / 0.16, 69.2 / 0.21, 46.9 / 0.21; Stable SGD 80.1 / 0.09, 70.8 / 0.10, 59.9 / 0.08; MC-SGD (replay used to stay on a low-loss linear path between the old and the new solution) 85.3 / 0.06, 82.3 / 0.08, 63.3 / 0.06; multi-task upper bound 89.5, 89.8, 68.8. Caveat from the paper: training longer on each task (20 instead of 5 epochs) pushed the solutions out of the connected low-curvature region. **[scratch-small] (full)** — [Mirzadeh et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04495)
- Replay in reinforcement learning: mixing new experience with replayed old experience plus behavioural cloning substantially reduces forgetting in Atari and DMLab, "even matching the performance of methods that require task identities"; a size-limited buffer with random discarding performs almost as well as an unbounded one (NeurIPS 2019). **[scratch] (abs)** — [Rolnick et al. 2018/2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)
- Large pre-trained models need very little replay: T0 (3B and 11B) trained on eight new generation tasks in sequence with a memory of 1% of previous data keeps 98.0% and 99.8% of the upper-bound performance; "at 0% rehearsal, the model catastrophically forgets", "with only 0.25% rehearsal we observe an almost perfect stability" (EMNLP 2022). **[PT-large] (full)** — [Scialom, Chakrabarty, Muresan 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12393)
- BERT-base on streams of text classification and question answering: no replay 18.4 accuracy / 53.1 F1; A-GEM 66.9 / 56.2; sparse replay (100 stored examples replayed every 10,000 new ones, a 1% rate) 57.8 / 57.9; replay plus test-time local adaptation 70.6 / 62.4; multi-task upper bound 73.6 / 67.8; storing only 10% of examples still gives 67.6 / 61.5. **[PT-small] (full)** — [de Masson d'Autume et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01076)
- Continual pre-training of a language model on new data: re-warming and re-decaying the learning rate combined with replay of previous data matches re-training from scratch on all data at the 405M scale (and for a weak shift at 10B), at a fraction of the compute. **[scratch] (abs; the replay percentage was not in the abstract)** — [Ibrahim et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763)

#### Regularisation, distillation and gradient projection without old data
- EWC: protects old tasks by "selectively slowing down learning on the weights important for those tasks"; shown on MNIST-based task sequences and Atari games. (That the penalty is quadratic and weighted by the diagonal Fisher information is from memory.) In the small-network benchmarks above EWC is clearly below tiny replay (for example 41.2 vs. 56.2 on Split CIFAR). **[scratch-small] (abs)** — [Kirkpatrick et al. 2016/2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796); [Chaudhry et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)
- Learning without Forgetting: uses only new-task data and aims to preserve the old capabilities; reported to perform similarly to multi-task learning that uses the old data. (That it does so by distilling the old model's outputs on the new data is from memory.) **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Li and Hoiem 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09282)
- Orthogonal Gradient Descent: projects new-task gradients onto a subspace in which the network's outputs on previous tasks do not change; needs no stored data. **[scratch-small] (abs)** — [Farajtabar et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07104)

#### Weight-space and task-vector approaches to continual learning
- Interpolating "between old and new model weights after each novel task", combined with experience replay, gives a "significant performance enhancement" over replay alone and a direct handle on the stability-plasticity trade-off. Details (models, permutation alignment, numbers) not read. **(abs)** — [Kozal et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04002)
- MagMax (ECCV 2024): fine-tune sequentially, form one task vector per stage relative to the pre-trained model, keep for each parameter the value of largest magnitude, apply with lambda = 0.5. CLIP ViT-B/16, class-incremental average over 14 scenarios: MagMax 74.50, LwF 72.36, TIES 71.84, averaging 71.75, EWC 70.79, zero-shot 67.21, joint training 87.38. Sequential fine-tuning improves every merging method compared with independent fine-tuning (averaging 71.75 -> 73.05, TIES 71.84 -> 74.37) because it reduces sign conflicts; forgetting on the first two tasks falls from 25.7% and 22.3% (sequential fine-tuning) to 9.8% and 1.7%. Nothing on models trained from scratch. **[PT-large] (full)** — [Marczak et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06322)
- Tangent Model Composition (ICCV 2023): component models fine-tuned in the tangent space of a pre-trained model can be added, scaled or subtracted for incremental learning, ensembling or unlearning; 4.2% better than ensembling non-linearly fine-tuned models at 2.5-10x lower inference cost; outperforms recent continual fine-tuning methods "almost uniformly" without a replay buffer. **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Liu and Soatto 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08114)
- Merging over time ("temporal model merging", the TIME framework) is organised along three axes: initialisation, deployment and merging technique. Findings not read. **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Dziadzio et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06712)
- Weight interpolation between an original and a fine-tuned model (WiSE-FT, numbers in Section 2) and the linear path between sequential and multi-task solutions of small networks (Mirzadeh et al., above) are the two results that make "interpolate old and new weights" plausible. **[PT-large; scratch-small]** — [Wortsman et al. 2021/2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01903); [Mirzadeh et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04495)

#### One adapter per skill, with routing
- O-LoRA learns each new task in a low-rank subspace kept orthogonal to those of earlier tasks, needs no replay data and adds few parameters (EMNLP 2023 findings). **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Wang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14152)
- A library of LoRA adapters built by clustering tasks, with a zero-shot router ("Arrow") that picks adapters per input, is reported to generalise better to new tasks on Phi-2 and Mistral; matching joint training is stated as the goal. **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Ostapenko et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11157)
- For tasks that need two skills at once, concatenated LoRAs with learned weights beat data mixing by 12% on average (Section 2). **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Prabhakar et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13025)

#### Knowledge editing
- ROME locates factual associations in mid-layer feed-forward modules by causal tracing and edits one fact with a rank-one weight update (NeurIPS 2022); MEMIT edits many facts at once and "scales up to thousands of associations for GPT-J (6B) and GPT-NeoX (20B)". **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Meng et al. 2022, ROME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262); [Meng et al. 2022, MEMIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229)
- Sequential editing breaks down (GPT-2 XL 1.5B and GPT-J 6B; ACL 2024 Findings): the model "continually forgets previously edited facts" and then collapses. With ROME the collapse "can come as early as 100 edits, or as late as 1000 edits"; with MEMIT it begins "after approximately 1400 edits" for GPT-J and "around 4000 edits" for GPT-2 XL; single "disabling edits" can leave the model unable to do any downstream task. **[PT-large] (full)** — [Gupta, Rao, Anumanchipalli 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07453)
- Editing harms general abilities: four editing methods on three LLMs and eight tasks show a significant drop in reasoning, inference and question answering, attributed to the edits "altering the original model weights excessively" (EMNLP 2024). **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Gu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04700)
- Learned editors: MEND trains small auxiliary networks that transform a fine-tuning gradient into a local edit, shown on T5, GPT, BERT and BART up to more than 10 billion parameters (ICLR 2022). "Editable Training" is a model-agnostic training technique that makes later edits cheap, evaluated on image classification and machine translation. **[PT-large] (abs)** — [Mitchell et al. 2021/2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11309); [Sinitsin et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00345)

### Inferences
- The observation in this system (training only on new rules: 54% -> 25%; mixing in half old data: 49%) is the standard replay result. The literature says the replay share can probably be much smaller than one half: small from-scratch networks were kept from collapse with one stored example per class, and large pre-trained models with 0.25-1%. A small model with little spare capacity may need more than a large one, so the share should be measured by a sweep (for example 50, 25, 10, 5, 1%), not assumed.
- The remaining loss (54 -> 49) is the kind of gap that weight interpolation between the model before and after the injection, or a path-constrained method like MC-SGD, is reported to close. Both rely on the new model being a short continuation of the old one; Mirzadeh et al. warn that long training on the new data leaves the connected region.
- A "new-rules task vector" (model trained on new rules minus the model before) scaled by a coefficient below one is the same line as that interpolation. For pre-trained models there is usually an intermediate point that is better on the combined objective than either end; whether a 12.4M from-scratch model has such a point is an empirical question that one sweep answers.
- Regularisation-only methods (EWC, Learning without Forgetting, orthogonal gradients) are attractive when old data cannot be kept. Here the old training data and the generator are available, so replay dominates and those methods are at best add-ons.
- Knowledge editing in the ROME/MEMIT sense is a poor fit: it rewrites subject-relation-object facts stored in mid-layer feed-forward modules of very large language models, it degrades after a few hundred to a few thousand edits, and no source shows it working on tiny models or on procedural knowledge.
- Per-skill adapters with routing add parameters and a routing decision. All evidence is for LLMs with large frozen backbones; for a 12.4M model, where most capacity is already in use, there is no evidence either way.

### Gaps
- No study was found on continual injection of new "rules" or skills into small program-synthesis transformers trained from scratch; the small-scale continual-learning evidence is image classification with MLPs and reduced ResNets.
- No evidence was found for ROME, MEMIT or MEND on models below GPT-2 scale or for non-factual knowledge; I could not confirm the model sizes used for "Editable Training" from its abstract.
- The details of Kozal et al. (weight interpolation with replay) and of the TIME framework's findings were not read. Pseudo-replay with generated data and LAMOL-style methods were not researched.
- The replay percentage used by Ibrahim et al. was not in the abstract.
