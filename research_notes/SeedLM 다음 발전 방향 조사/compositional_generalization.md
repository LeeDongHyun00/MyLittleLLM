# Compositional generalization for small from-scratch language-to-program models (no pretrained weights, few thousand natural pairs)

Reading guide. Research date: November 2026. Tags used on every result: **[NP]** = no pretrained model in the reported system; **[PT]** = a pretrained model is used (the component is named). "Acc" is exact-match accuracy in % unless stated. Numbers were read from the primary papers (arXiv / ACL Anthology / PMC full text, two PDFs converted locally with pdftotext); where a number only exists in a figure, or a source could not be opened, that is said explicitly. Benchmarks: SCAN (synthetic commands to actions), COGS (synthetic English to logical form; 24,155 train, 21,000 generalization examples), CFQ (239,357 synthetic English questions to SPARQL; MCD = maximum compound divergence splits), GeoQuery (880 real questions), SMCalFlow-CS (about 25k real calendar utterances plus 16 to 128 cross-skill examples).

## 1. Grammar and lexicon induction approaches trained from scratch (NQG, latent QCFG, LeAR, lexicon learning, span-based parsing, stack machines, newer work through 2026)

### Takeaway
From-scratch grammar and lexicon methods reach 96 to 100% on SCAN, 82 to 99.5% on COGS and (LeAR) 90.9% on CFQ-MCD, but every one of them buys this with a rigid word-to-symbol alignment assumption, and their coverage collapses on real language: NQG covers 80% of standard GeoQuery but 44% of its compositional splits and about 1% of Spider, and CSL alone scores 5 to 11% on SMCalFlow-CS. The parts that transfer to a small natural-language-to-program model are the lexicon/copy channel (COGS 51 to 82% for an LSTM) and the use of an induced or hand-built grammar as a data sampler, not the grammar parsers themselves.

### Cited Findings

#### Induced synchronous grammars (NQG, neural QCFG, CSL)
- NQG (Shaw et al., ACL 2021) induces a quasi-synchronous CFG from source-target pairs with a greedy minimum-description-length search; it needs no word alignments and no pre-specified grammar, but its rule scorer uses BERT-Tiny (4.4M, SCAN/Spider) or BERT-Base (GeoQuery), so it is **[PT-scorer]**. NQG alone: SCAN jump / turn-left / length / MCD 100/100/100/100; GeoQuery standard / template / length / TMCD 76.8 / 61.9 / 37.4 / 41.1; Spider random / template / length / TMCD 1.3 / 0.5 / 0.0 / 0.5 — [Shaw et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12725)
- NQG coverage (share of test inputs with any derivation): GeoQuery 80.2 / 64.5 / 43.3 / 43.7, Spider 1.5 / 0.5 / 0.0 / 0.6; precision on covered inputs stays high (GeoQuery 95.7 / 95.8 / 86.4 / 94.1). The authors say induction is "particularly unsuccessful for Spider". Falling back to T5 (NQG-T5-Base, **[PT]**) gives GeoQuery 92.9 / 88.8 / 52.2 / 56.6 versus T5-Base 92.9 / 87.0 / 39.1 / 54.3 — [Shaw et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12725)
- A later paper lists NQG's GeoQuery length result as ".37/.26", the second value "from a replication study" (the ReScience replication by Sun, Williams and Hupkes; I could not open its tables) — [Soulos et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14076); [replication code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/CompGenRep_MLRC2022)
- Neural QCFG (Kim, NeurIPS 2021) **[NP]**: SCAN simple 96.9, add-jump 96.8, around-right 98.7, length 95.7. No alignments and no source parser are needed (the source parser is learned jointly). Cost of the inside algorithm is O(|N|(|N|+|P|)^2 S^3 T^3); on realistic machine translation the author "quickly ran into memory issues" even on four 32GB GPUs, small-scale English-French BLEU was 23.5 (26.8 with a BiLSTM) versus 30.4 for a Transformer, and the author calls the MT results "largely negative" — [Kim 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01135)
- CSL (Qiu et al., NAACL 2022) **[NP as a parser]**: a QCFG induced by parallel greedy search plus a simple latent-state model with scalar parameters. CSL alone: SCAN jump / left / length / MCD 100/100/100/100; COGS 99.5 (after converting COGS to a variable-free form, because QCFGs cannot handle logical variables); GeoQuery standard / template / TMCD / length 74.3 / 58.9 / 39.9 / 26.6; SMCalFlow-CS cross-domain 5.3 / 9.8 / 11.3 at 8/16/32 shots (single-domain 26.1 / 25.2 / 26.7). Only 29.0% of GeoQuery-length test inputs are covered by the grammar — [Qiu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07610)

#### Latent tree composition with a lexicon (LeAR, span-based parsing)
- LeAR (Liu et al., Findings of ACL 2021) **[NP]**: CFQ MCD mean 90.9 ± 1.2 (MCD1/2/3 91.7 / 89.2 / 91.7) versus Transformer 17.9, T5-11B 40.9, HPD 67.3; COGS 97.7 ± 0.7 (Transformer 35 ± 6); GeoQuery query split of Finegan-Dollak et al. 84.1 versus SpanBasedSP 82.2 and Seq2Seq 46.0. Ablations on CFQ: without Tree-LSTM 30.4, without curriculum 71.9 ± 15.4; on COGS without Tree-LSTM 80.7 — [Liu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06516)
- LeAR's requirements: semantic operations predefined per domain ("requires some efforts from domain experts"), a phrase table from GIZA++ word alignment (CFQ) or handcrafted (GeoQuery), REINFORCE training, and a "primitive alignment" assumption that every meaning primitive aligns to a span (the paper's counterexample: "Obama's brother"). COGS can be trained on "one CPU" — [Liu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06516)
- SpanBasedSP (Herzig and Berant, ACL 2021) **[PT encoder: BERT-base]**: SCAN-SP right / around-right 100 / 100 (Seq2Seq 11.6 / 0.0); CLOSURE 98.8 after training on 10K CLEVR examples (baselines use 695K); GeoQuery iid / template / length 86.1 / 82.2 / 63.6 versus Seq2Seq 78.5 / 46.0 / 24.3 and BART 87.1 / 67.0 / 19.3. Trees are latent (hard-EM with CKY, O(n^4) with the ternary join rule); a lexicon with at most two manual phrases per predicate is required, and removing it drops GeoQuery length from 63.6 to 41.4. It assumes a one-to-one mapping between domain constants and phrases — [Herzig and Berant 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06040)

#### Lexicon learning (Akyurek and Andreas 2021)
- LSTM with a lexical translation channel **[NP]** (a gate interpolates the decoder's own distribution with attention-weighted entries of a word-to-symbol lexicon; with an identity lexicon it reduces to a copy mechanism): COGS 0.51 to 0.82 (LSTM with plain copy 0.66); SCAN jump 0.00 to 0.92 ("Simple" lexicon) or 0.95 (PMI); SCAN around-right 0.09 to 0.95 (Simple; PMI 0.02, IBM Model 2 0.00); Colors 0.00 to 0.79; English-Chinese MT 24.18 to 25.49 BLEU (IBM Model 2 lexicon), 1-shot words 17.47 to 19.62. Model: 2-layer LSTM, 512 units. Lexicons come from IBM Model 2 (FastAlign), PMI, a Bayesian model, or a rule ("Simple") based on necessary/sufficient co-occurrence with frequency threshold 3 — [Akyurek and Andreas 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03993)
- Stated limits: structural words with no output token ("thrice") must still be learned by the ordinary decoder, and "generalization to longer sequences remains" open. In a per-type breakdown the lexicon LSTM gets 96 on COGS lexical cases but 0 / 0 / 1 on the three structural cases — [Akyurek and Andreas 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03993); [Lindemann et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16954)

#### Neural-symbolic machines and rule synthesis
- NeSS (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2020) **[NP]**: SCAN length / around-right / jump / simple 100/100/100/100; few-shot instruction task 100 (humans 84.3); compositional MT 100; context-free grammar parsing 100% up to test length 5000. Training needs execution-trace search (256-step budget), a 6-lesson curriculum on SCAN, and "operational equivalence" category predictors (without them around-right falls to about 20% in 3 of 5 runs). The authors "did not run experiments" on natural-language datasets — [Chen et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06662)
- LANE (Liu et al. 2020) is reported at 100 on SCAN jump, length and MCD — [as tabulated by Shaw et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12725)
- Rule synthesis from support examples (Nye et al., NeurIPS 2020) **[NP]**: a neural proposer writes a whole grammar, candidates are executed on the support examples and search stops at the first consistent one; SCAN length / simple / jump / right 100/100/100/100 with search but 0.0 / 13.3 / 3.5 / 0.0 without search, using under 2% of the training data; search takes 33.7 to 74.6 s and 1,296 to 2,993 candidate grammars; the meta-grammar is hand-designed — [Nye et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05562)
- NSR (Li et al.; usually cited as ICLR 2024, but the venue is not shown on the page I read) **[NP for symbol inputs]**: SCAN 100 on all four splits, PCFG iid / systematicity / productivity 100/100/100 (NeSS about 0 on PCFG), HINT average 76.0 versus Transformer 53.1; trained with a deduction-abduction search; the authors call the translation experiment a "proof-of-concept" — [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01603)
- Compositional Program Generation (Klinger et al. 2023) reports perfect SCAN and COGS from 14 and 22 examples, but requires a context-free grammar of the input language and a word-to-meaning dictionary (abstract-level verification only) — [Klinger et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16467)

#### Structure-aware parsers on COGS, CFQ and SLOG
- Petit, Corro, Yvon (EMNLP 2023) **[NP]**: supertagging plus graph parsing gets COGS overall 98.1, object-to-subject PP 75.0, PP recursion 100, CP recursion 100, lexical 99.1, versus a standard graph parser at 84.1 overall with 0 on both recursions. Needs an integer linear program (CPLEX) and "cannot predict supertags unseen during training" — [Petit et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14124)
- AM parser (Weissenhorn et al. 2022): without BERT **[NP]** 59.9 ± 2.7 (trained on the standard set) and 91.1 ± 2.3 (train100 set); with BERT-Large embeddings **[PT]** 79.6 ± 6.4 and 93.6; with BERT and relative distances 78.3 ± 22.9 and 98.4. Requires a hand-built decomposition of logical forms into graph fragments and alignment heuristics — [Weissenhorn et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11937)
- Multiset tagging plus latent permutations (Lindemann et al., ACL 2023): with RoBERTa **[PT]** COGS total 92 ± 2 (PP recursion 85, CP recursion 79, object-to-subject 9). The non-pretrained variant (BiLSTM plus GloVe) gets ATIS length 38.79 versus LSTM 4.95, Transformer 1.15 and BART-base 19.03, and Okapi length splits calendar / document / email 65.6 / 41.4 / 47.6 versus BART-base 36.7 / 0.6 / 20.5. Initialised from IBM Model 1 alignments — [Lindemann et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16954)
- Structured reordering (ReMoto; Wang, Lapata, Titov, NeurIPS 2021) **[NP]**: SCAN-SP length 100 (Seq2Seq 13.9); GeoQuery English iid / template / length 75.2 / 43.2 / 23.2 versus Seq2Seq 75.7 / 38.8 / 21.8 — [Wang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03257)
- LAGr (Jambor and Bahdanau, ACL 2022) **[NP]**: COGS 82.5 ± 2.9 (strongly supervised, separate encoders); CFQ MCD mean 39.5 weakly supervised (MCD1/2/3 62.8 / 30.3 / 25.4) versus Transformer 17.9 and a Transformer on simplified SPARQL 31.4; weakly supervised COGS training converges in only about 50% of runs — [Jambor and Bahdanau 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07572)
- Edge Transformer (Bergen et al., NeurIPS 2021) **[NP]**: CFQ MCD mean 24.69 ± 1.27, COGS 87.4 ± 0.4; O(n^3) memory and time — [Bergen et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00578)
- Grounded Graph Decoding (Gai et al. 2021) **[NP, 0.3M parameters]**: CFQ MCD1/2/3 97.9 ± 0.2 / 47.1 ± 10.4 / 50.8 ± 17.2 without dev tuning; relies on anonymised entities and part-of-speech grouping of "A and B" phrases and was evaluated only on CFQ — [Gai et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03642)
- HPD (Guo et al. 2020) is cited at CFQ MCD mean 67.3 ± 4.1 (run average) and 69.0 (79.6 / 59.6 / 67.8, with dev tuning); it uses sketch prediction and lexicons — [Liu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06516); [Gai et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03642); [Jambor and Bahdanau 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07572)
- Sparse Differentiable Tree Machine (Soulos et al., NeurIPS 2024) **[NP, about 1M parameters]**: SCAN 0-shot lexical .99, length .75, template .95, MCD .03 (best of 5 runs; SCAN iid mean .80 ± .39); GeoQuery iid / length / template / TMCD .73 / .20 / .20 / .36 versus a Transformer at .88 / .26 / .79 / .40. The authors say it "struggles with MCD and Template shifts, as well as the extremely small GeoQuery dataset" — [Soulos et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14076)
- SLOG (Li et al., EMNLP 2023; COGS extended with 17 structural cases): Transformer from scratch **[NP]** 27.1 ± 2.0, T5-base **[PT]** 40.6 ± 1.0, AM parser 70.8 ± 4.3 with 0.0 on some gap constructions — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15040)
- Two 2026 single-author preprints on SLOG (a neural cellular automaton at 67.3%, and CCG directed types with BERT at 75.9%) were both **withdrawn** in August 2026 because the reported exact match "was computed against a final-state proxy"; their numbers should not be used — [arXiv 2604.26157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26157); [arXiv 2607.02307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02307)

#### SMCalFlow-CS and what natural language does to these methods
- SMCalFlow-CS (Yin et al., NAACL 2021): 25,404 training examples (24,763 event creation, 641 org chart) plus 16 to 128 cross-skill examples. BERT2Seq **[PT encoder]** cross-skill accuracy 33.6 / 53.5 / 64.2 / 71.3 at 16 / 32 / 64 / 128 examples; adding span-level attention supervision from GIZA++ (IBM Model 4) alignments gives 46.8 / 61.7 / 66.9 / 74.3 — [Yin et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.225/)
- In the 8/16/32-shot variant: T5 **[PT]** 34.7 / 44.7 / 59.0, T5 + CSL augmentation 51.6 / 61.4 / 70.4, CSL alone **[NP]** 5.3 / 9.8 / 11.3 — [Qiu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07610)
- Architectures specialised for SCAN do not carry over to more natural data: CGPS scores 98.8 on SCAN add-jump but 7.1 on CFQ MCD (below the LSTM baseline's 14.9), Neural Shuffle-Exchange 2.8 — [Furrer et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08970)
- A from-scratch language-and-examples-to-program system that does cope with human wording is LAPS (Wong et al., ICML 2021) **[NP]**: an IBM Model 4 translation model between words and program primitives (Moses), a joint prior over programs and descriptions, and a language-conditioned search model (64-unit GRU) trained on sampled program-description pairs. Test tasks solved, DreamCoder without language versus the three LAPS variants: string editing 33.4 versus 52.2 / 57.0 / 54.6; graphics best run 49.55 versus 92.79 / 86.49 / 98.19 (mean over runs 42.64 versus 52.93 / 80.18 / 81.98); scenes mean 73.9 versus 88.1 / 82.3 / 95.9. A multimodal baseline that just conditions a neural synthesizer on language without the translation model gets 46.0 / 26.12 / 49.5. Trained on synthetic language and tested on human language: 54.6 / 89.2 / 97.4. Trained and tested on human language: 48.6 / 58.55 / 95.6 (the scenes figures in these two rows come from the table's curriculum column, where DreamCoder without language is 67.8) — [Wong et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11053)
- Training on grammar-generated-then-paraphrased data and testing on real user questions costs 86.3 to 61.9 (GeoQuery) and 83.4 to 40.8 (Scholar); about 9 points of this is "only due to the language mismatch", the rest because the grammar's programs cover only 76.5% / 63.7% of natural program templates — [Herzig and Berant 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09940)
- Projecting each natural sentence onto its nearest grammar-generated sentence before parsing reaches 0.37 logical-form accuracy on Overnight with zero real training data, versus 0.19 for a synthetic-only seq2seq and 0.27 for a seq2seq trained on real data; the projection uses bert-base-uncased embeddings **[PT]** — [Marzoev et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13645)

### Inferences
- The target system already owns what these papers induce: descriptions "composed from per-operator phrases" are a hand-built synchronous grammar with perfect alignments on 300k programs. Grammar induction on the roughly 6k natural pairs would land in the Spider regime (780 output symbols, free wording, coverage near zero), so NQG/CSL/neural-QCFG as the main parser is not worth building. Their cost (cubic or worse dynamic programs) is not the obstacle for 9-token programs; coverage is.
- The transferable mechanism is the lexical channel of Akyurek and Andreas: a second output distribution that proposes an operator because a word in the description is aligned to it, independent of the parent token. This attacks failure mode 1 directly (a token that creates an unseen parent-to-child link can become the top choice through the lexical path). The literature says to expect it to fix lexical novelty (COGS lexical 96%) and nothing structural (0 to 1%).
- LeAR, SpanBasedSP and NeSS all need each program primitive to be traceable to a span. One-sentence task descriptions written by people often name the goal, not each operator, so the "primitive alignment" assumption will hold for the templated data and only partly for natural tasks. A latent bottom-up composer in the LeAR style is therefore a risky main model, but its ingredients (alignment-derived phrase table from GIZA++-style models, typed composition, curriculum from short to long) are cheap to reuse as supervision.
- The split "predict the multiset of output symbols, then arrange them" (Lindemann et al.) and "predict supertags, then solve a matching problem" (Petit et al.) maps naturally onto this system: predict the bag of operators from the description, then let typed enumeration plus the execution check on the example arrange them. The arrangement step never needs to have seen a link, and the candidate space under a fixed bag is far smaller than under a broadened link prior.
- LAPS is the closest published analogue of the whole setting (from-scratch, descriptions plus examples, synthetic and human wording). Its result pattern (synthetic-language training transferring to human test language at 54.6 / 89.2 / 97.4, a classical IBM alignment model being enough with very few annotated programs) suggests that a statistical word-to-operator lexicon is a realistic component at this data scale.

### Gaps
- I found no result for any [NP] method on SMCalFlow-CS in its standard form; published numbers use BERT, T5 or Codex. The only from-scratch SMCalFlow-CS figures are from a data-factor study on a 25% subset (see section 5).
- CFQ, COGS and SCAN use synthetic language. Evidence for [NP] grammar/lexicon methods on real wording is limited to GeoQuery (880 examples), ATIS, Okapi and LAPS's three small domains.
- I could not open the tables of the ReScience replication of NQG-T5 and neural QCFG, so the size of the replication gap (.37 versus .26 on GeoQuery length) rests on a secondary citation.
- No paper found studies grammar or lexicon induction with an output vocabulary of several hundred library operators.

## 2. Data augmentation by aligned recombination (Jia and Liang, GECA, recombine-and-resample, SUBS, SpanSub, CSL, LexSym, Prim2PrimX, mixing methods, program-first synthesis)

### Takeaway
Aligned recombination is the family with the largest from-scratch gains on small real-language data (GeoQuery compositional split, LSTM: 58.6 to 72 with automatically induced span trees, 78 to 81 with near-gold span trees, 80.2 with lexical swaps), but every method needs a source of span-to-subtree alignments plus exchangeability classes, and the documented failures all point the same way: recombination without tight constraints lowers accuracy (GECA takes COGS from 55.4 to 48.0 and "can over-generate incorrect examples"), learned recombination "has a high error rate", directly mixing synthesized data into training "is indeed harmful", and the same augmentation that takes SCAN from 3.5 to 99.7 moves GeoQuery only from 42.4 to 47.9.

### Cited Findings

#### How each method aligns utterance pieces with program pieces, and what it gains without pretraining
- Jia and Liang (ACL 2016) **[NP]** induce a synchronous CFG with hand-specified strategies: AbsEntities (a token that string-matches an entity in the logical form is replaced by its type on both sides), AbsWholePhrases (a phrase whose logical form evaluates to a set of that type becomes a typed slot), Concat-k (k examples joined); ATIS additionally uses an external phrase lexicon. As many recombinant examples as real ones are sampled each epoch. GeoQuery 85.0 to 89.3 (AWP+AE+C2), ATIS 76.3 to 83.3 (AE+C3), Overnight 75.8 to 77.5. Attention-based copying alone gives GeoQuery 74.6 to 85.0 and ATIS 69.9 to 76.3 but Overnight 76.7 to 75.8 — [Jia and Liang 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03622)
- GECA (Andreas, ACL 2020) **[NP]** uses no alignment at all: any fragment that appears in the same "environment" (the rest of the concatenated input-output string) as another fragment may replace it wherever that other fragment occurs; matching is exact-string. SCAN jump 0.00 to 0.87, around-right 0.00 to 0.82 (395 new examples from 6 fragments on jump); GeoQuery logical forms query split 0.62 to 0.65 and question split 0.76 to 0.78; GeoQuery SQL query split 0.39 to 0.49 (1,055 new examples); Scholar unchanged (0.03 to 0.03 and 0.57 to 0.56). Low-resource language-model perplexity improves by 0 to 4 points — [Andreas 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09545)
- Recombine-and-resample (Akyurek, Akyurek, Andreas, ICLR 2021) **[NP]** replaces rules with a neural generator that copies from one or two retrieved prototypes (neighbours by token-set difference and edit distance), then keeps only samples containing rare tokens ("resampling"). SCAN jump 0.87 (0.88 without resampling), around-right 0.75 (0.82 without); a generator without prototypes 0.00; without copying 0.00. Morphology (future/past tense F1): Spanish 0.66 to 0.72, Swahili 0.75 to 0.85, Turkish 0.69 to 0.77 — [Akyurek et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03706)
- SUBS (Yang, Zhang, Yang, NAACL 2022) takes span trees from the Herzig-Berant span parser (gold = semi-automatically annotated; induced = produced by the trained parser) and exchanges subtrees whose roots share a hand-defined category (on GeoQuery, the enclosing predicate). LSTM **[NP]** GeoQuery query split 0.58 to 0.72 (induced trees) and 0.79 (gold trees), versus Jia-Liang SCFG 0.68 and GECA 0.60; question split 0.75 to 0.79 / 0.81. BART **[PT]** 0.85 to 0.85 (induced) / 0.88 (gold). SUBS produces 29,039 augmented GeoQuery instances versus GECA's 804, with exchanged segments averaging 5.99 tokens versus 1.93 — [Yang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01538)
- SpanSub (Li, Wei, Lian, ACL 2023) exchanges aligned spans whose words fall in the same equivalence class. Alignment sources: on SCAN an extension of the "Simple" lexicon rule from single words to fragments (at most 3 input words, 8 output tokens); on COGS the tree intermediate representation of Ontanon et al.; on GeoQuery the Herzig-Berant span trees with some gold-span corrections. Word classes come from shared contexts (plus a one-layer LSTM masked-reconstruction model on GeoQuery). LSTM **[NP]**: SCAN jump 1.3 to 100.0, around-right 10.2 to 99.9, MCD1/2/3 8.9 / 11.9 / 6.0 to 63.4 / 72.9 / 74.0; COGS 55.4 to 91.8; GeoQuery question / query 75.2 / 58.6 to 82.4 / 81.4 (LexSym 81.6 / 80.2, SUBS 80.5 / 77.7, GECA 76.8 / 60.6). Transformer **[NP]** SCAN MCD 1.7 / 4.3 / 4.4 to 24.8 / 79.4 / 61.3. BART **[PT]** GeoQuery query 71.9 to 89.5 — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02840)
- L2S2 / LCS (the learned version) trains the augmenter with Gumbel-softmax to pick the substitutions that maximise the parser's loss; it adds 0 to 6 points over fixed sampling (SCAN MCD 67.4 / 73.0 / 80.2; COGS 92.3). The 2025 extension CompSub + LCS **[NP]** reports SCAN length 12.5 to 85.6 (97.1 with LCS) and a COGS breakdown of lexical 99.1, object-PP-to-subject-PP 91.8, PP recursion 45.0, CP recursion 7.2 (plain seq2seq 69.3 / 0.0 / 0.0 / 0.9; LexSym 95.3 / 0.0 / 0.0 / 0.7) — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02840); [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20834)
- CSL augmentation (Qiu et al. 2022) samples 100,000 synthetic pairs from the induced QCFG and fine-tunes T5 **[PT]**: SCAN left 62.0 to 100.0, length 14.4 to 99.2, MCD 15.4 to 99.4; COGS 89.8 to 99.5; GeoQuery template / TMCD / length 84.8 / 69.2 / 41.8 to 89.3 / 74.9 / 67.8; SMCalFlow-CS cross-domain 34.7 / 44.7 / 59.0 to 51.6 / 61.4 / 70.4. No non-pretrained target model is reported — [Qiu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07610)
- LexSym (Akyurek and Andreas, ACL 2023) needs only a token-level lexicon (IBM Model 2 or the "Simple" rule), infers word types by clustering contexts, and swaps two same-type lexicon entries everywhere in an example (a "homomorphism"). LSTM **[NP]**: SCAN jump 0.41 to 100.00, around-right 8.65 to 99.51, COGS 61.13 to 81.86, ALCHEMY 41.72 to 45.85; a from-scratch VQA transformer on CLEVR-CoGenT 73.3 to 85.9 (CLEVR iid 93.6 to 92.0). T5 **[PT]** COGS 83.30 to 83.62 — [Akyurek and Andreas 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12926)
- Prim2PrimX (Jiang, Zhou, Bansal, EMNLP 2022) **[NP]** finds primitives with a rule-based alignment lexicon and clones each into new numbered primitives on both sides ("walk0 ... WALK0"; 5 clones per primitive on SCAN, 2 on COGS). COGS Transformer 76.14 to 80.07, 81.12 with mutual-exclusivity training (MET: an unlikelihood loss that penalises producing the same target when one input word is swapped); MET alone 80.64. SCAN around-right LSTM 10.33 to 97.67; SCAN jump Transformer 3.49 to 63.65 — [Jiang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15578)
- SeqMix (Guo, Kim, Rush, EMNLP 2020) needs no alignment: it interpolates two input-output pairs with a Beta-distributed weight. SCAN jump 0 to 49 (98 on top of GECA), around-right 0 to 0, GeoQuery SQL query split 39 to 43 (52 on top of GECA), IWSLT German-English 34.7 to 36.2 BLEU; the authors call it "a crude approximation" (the paper does not state explicitly that models are trained from scratch; they are small LSTMs and standard fairseq Transformers) — [Guo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09039)
- TreeMix (Zhang, Yang, Yang, NAACL 2022) mixes constituency-parse subtrees between sentences and is evaluated on text classification and SCAN; the abstract gives no numbers and I did not verify which models it uses — [Zhang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06153)
- A 2025 preprint mines variable-slot templates ("pseudo-constructions") from SCAN training data and reports add-jump 47.8 and around-right 20.3 (abstract only; baselines not given) — [Katrapati and Shrivastava 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20074)

#### Program-first synthesis: sample programs, then obtain language
- Sampling meaning representations from a grammar and back-translating them with a fine-tuned T5 (Yao and Koller, NAACL 2024) **[PT]**: on COGS, PP recursion 24.1 (no augmentation) to 51.2 (programs sampled from a grammar fitted to the training set) to 87.8 (uniform grammar) to 96.7 (test-distribution grammar); CP recursion 32.3 / 43.5 / 50.7 / 95.1; GeoQuery length exact match 35.8 / 56.1 / 60.4 / 60.1; SCAN length 4.4 / 8.1 / 60.5 / 60.5. "The grammar estimated on the train set tends to produce simple structures". Back-translation itself is noisy (exact match 30.9 on COGS structural cases, 19.6 on GeoQuery template) and still helps — [Yao and Koller 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09815)
- Learning to Synthesize Data (Wang et al., NAACL 2021) **[PT]**: programs from a learned PCFG, questions from BART; GeoQuery query split 49.5 to 62.1 (54.8 with a uniform instead of a trained PCFG, 53.9 without pretrained BART); Spider execution 69.4 to 72.5. Preliminary experiments found that "directly mixing the synthesized data with the original training data is indeed harmful", so synthetic data is used for a pre-training stage followed by fine-tuning on real data — [Wang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05827)
- Iterative back-translation with unpaired programs (Guo et al., AAAI 2021) **[NP, GRU with attention]**: CFQ MCD1/2/3 32.6 / 6.0 / 9.5 to 64.8 / 57.8 / 64.6 with 30% of the dev set's questions and queries as unpaired data ("mono30", the setting the authors call realistic), 83.2 / 71.5 / 81.3 with all dev data, 88.4 / 81.6 / 88.2 when test data are used transductively; curriculum variant mean 68.3. SCAN MCD 18.7 / 32.0 / 42.4 to 64.3 / 80.8 / 52.2. Initial pseudo-pairs were only 16.0% accurate and still helped; on-the-fly regeneration of pseudo-inputs matters (33.3 versus 20.1). The unpaired programs come from the evaluation distribution, not from a generic sampler — [Guo et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04276)
- Structurally diverse sampling from a synthetic pool (Oren et al., EMNLP 2021) **[PT, BART-base]**: Schema2QA compositional exact match 20.1 with no synthetic data, 22.2 with 5K uniformly sampled synthetic examples, 37.7 with 1M, 41.4 with 5K sampled uniformly over abstract program templates (UAT; "a 200x improvement in data efficiency"), 43.0 with 120K; GECA 19.7 — [Oren et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02575)

#### Documented failure modes
- Over-generation of wrong pairs. GECA's own manual checks: 96% grammatical / 98% semantically accurate on GeoQuery, but only 79% / 51% on English text; the paper calls its substitution principle "obviously wrong" as a general claim and notes it ignores constituent boundaries. Recombine-and-resample: of 400 samples, 40% (around-right) and 74% (jump) of novel samples were exactly correct, 14% for Turkish; "The augmentation procedure has a high error rate!" — [Andreas 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09545); [Akyurek et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03706)
- Augmentation that lowers accuracy. GECA on COGS: 55.4 to 48.0 (LSTM), 0.51 to 0.48; with T5 on SCAN left 62.0 to 57.6, SCAN length 14.4 to 10.5, GeoQuery template 84.8 to 82.8 and TMCD 69.2 to 66.5, which Qiu et al. attribute to the fact that it "can over-generate incorrect examples". SpanSub's authors report that noise slightly hurts the GeoQuery iid split for BART (90.6 versus 91.8 for SUBS) and name reducing it as future work. CSL augmentation slightly lowers SMCalFlow-CS single-domain accuracy (84.7 to 83.5) — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02840); [Akyurek and Andreas 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03993); [Qiu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07610)
- Errors move instead of disappearing. With template-uniform synthetic data, "UAT reduces structural errors but increases linking errors" (linking is the most common error type). With Prim2PrimX, primitive errors fall from 22.99% to 0.52% of test cases but structural errors only from 73.80% to 35.82% — [Oren et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02575); [Jiang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15578)
- Gains shrink on real language and vanish for pretrained models. The same primitive augmentation at 2x / 20x / 200x: SCAN jump 3.49 to 77.37 / 99.68 / 99.93, but GeoQuery query split 42.37 to 45.92 / 47.85 / 45.70 (from-scratch 3-layer Transformer); for T5-base it gave "no consistent improvement" — [Zhou et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04420)
- Where the sampler looks matters more than how much it produces. Learned recombination without resampling "generate[s] almost no examples of rare tags"; a train-fitted grammar "tends to produce simple structures"; a PCFG with independent rules produces invalid SQL — [Akyurek et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03706); [Yao and Koller 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09815); [Wang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05827)
- Cost. GECA was reported as computationally infeasible on COGS and SMCalFlow-CS and on translation data — [Qiu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07610); [Guo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09039)
- The synthetic-data distribution in program synthesis has the same effect: a Karel synthesizer at 73.52% drops to 27.9% when test inputs are resampled uniformly over a few salient variables; training on data made uniform over those variables gives 62.78 to 80.19% on narrow test distributions where the baseline gets 0.04 to 24.30% — [Shin et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12345)

### Inferences
- The system's failed experiments (random subtree grafting with templated words; 30k mixed programs) sit at the GECA end of this spectrum: broad, weakly constrained, and worded synthetically. The papers where recombination works keep three things tight: (a) what may be exchanged (same type or context class, aligned span and subtree moved together), (b) where sampling mass goes (resampling toward rare items, uniform over abstract templates, uniform grammar), and (c) how synthetic data enters training (pre-train then fine-tune on real pairs, not mixing). The grafting experiment appears to have had none of the three for the natural-language side.
- Unlike GECA or recombine-and-resample, grafted pairs in this system are exact (the program runs, the description is composed), so label noise is not the mechanism of the observed harm. The mechanism that fits is that templated-word augmentation teaches the acceptability of a link, not which natural wording calls for it; the output prior widens while grounding stays flat, and with a single input-output example more type-valid programs pass. None of the papers above measures this, because they score exact match against a gold program, not consistency with examples.
- The alignment prerequisites of SUBS and SpanSub are free on the 300k templated programs. For the natural pairs they can be induced the way Yin et al. and LeAR do it: token alignments from an IBM-style model or the "Simple" co-occurrence rule, then sub-program-to-contiguous-span extraction using the program tree. Recombining natural pairs with each other (span and subtree exchanged together, root types equal, result executed to regenerate the example) is the step that puts natural wording into new structural contexts; that is where the 58.6 to 72 to 81 GeoQuery gains came from.
- "Unlimited synthetic programs" plus an LLM teacher that can write descriptions is the Yao-Koller / Oren setting with a better back-translator. The literature's design rule is to sample programs uniformly over abstract templates or structures (not from the training distribution, not uniformly over programs) and to expect roughly a doubling of compositional accuracy at best on real-language test sets (20.1 to 41.4), with linking errors becoming the bottleneck.
- Iterative back-translation is the only from-scratch recipe that uses unpaired programs at scale, and a from-scratch describer trained on 6k pairs could play the back-translator if the teacher is not to be used. Its headline gains (CFQ 16 to 62) used unpaired data drawn from the evaluation distribution, so they are an upper bound.

### Gaps
- No paper found measures how augmentation changes the number of wrong programs that satisfy input-output examples; failure mode 2 of the target system has no direct precedent in this literature.
- CSL augmentation, Yao-Koller sampling, Oren et al. and Wang et al. are only reported with pretrained parsers; their from-scratch effect is unknown. The closest from-scratch evidence (Zhou et al.) shows +3 to +5 points on GeoQuery.
- I did not verify TreeMix's models or numbers beyond the abstract, and SeqMix does not state explicitly that its models are trained from scratch.
- No aligned-recombination study was found on a code domain with hundreds of library operators.

## 3. Meta-learning for compositionality (Lake 2019, Conklin et al. 2021, MLC in Nature 2023, follow-ups) and tiny models that use exemplars in context

### Takeaway
Meta-learning works here by turning word-to-meaning binding into something the model reads from context: models of 1.4M (MLC) to 5.7M (Compositional-ARC) parameters reach under 1% error on SCAN add-jump / around-right and COGS lexical cases, but they score 100% error on length and structural splits, and measured transfer to real wording is small (from-scratch meta-in-context training: GeoQuery TMCD 37.4 to 40.8; MAML: +8 points on COGS, synthetic only). Small from-scratch models can be trained to use exemplars, but the evidence says the exemplars must jointly cover the pieces the query needs (gSCAN split H: 0.17 with similarity retrieval, 0.56 with coverage retrieval, 0.80 with generated covering supports) and that nearest-neighbour supports during training cause over-copying.

### Cited Findings

#### How episodes are built and how large the models are
- Meta seq2seq (Lake, NeurIPS 2019) **[NP]**: two-layer biLSTM encoders and LSTM decoder with 200 units; support items are stored in a key-value memory that the query attends to. Add-jump episodes: 4 primitives randomly re-assigned to 4 meanings (23 permutations), 20 support and 20 query commands, 10,000 episodes, at test time the support is just the four isolated primitives. Results: add-jump 99.95, around-right 99.96, length 16.64; standard seq2seq 0.03; without the support loss 5.43. The paper states "generalizing to longer output sequences remains unsolved" — [Lake 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05381)
- MLC (Lake and Baroni, Nature 2023) **[NP]**: a standard encoder-decoder Transformer, 3 + 3 layers, 8 heads, embedding 128, feed-forward 512, "about 1.4 million parameters". Human-task training: 100,000 episodes, each from a freshly sampled interpretation grammar (4 primitive rules, 3 function rules, 8 input and 6 output symbols), up to 14 study examples and 10 queries, an auxiliary copy task on the study examples, 50 epochs. Benchmark version: episodes sample 10 study examples and 2 queries (SCAN) or 8 and 2 (COGS) from the ordinary training corpus and apply a word-type permutation with probability 0.95 (SCAN: input primitives and output actions; COGS: 406 common nouns, 103 proper nouns, 22 dative verbs, 21 infinitive verbs) — [Lake and Baroni 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06668-3); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620072)
- MLC results (error rate, MLC versus basic seq2seq): SCAN add-jump 0.22% versus 99.27%; around-right 0.04% versus 51.13%; opposite-right 0.06% versus 100.00%; COGS lexical generalization 0.87% versus 6.08%. On SCAN length and the three COGS structural types MLC's error is 100%. Humans gave the algebraic answer in 80.7% of few-shot cases; MLC with sampling 82.4%. Stated limits: it does not handle "unpracticed forms of generalization", lacks a mechanism for emitting new symbols, and is untested on the full complexity of natural language — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620072)
- A 2025/2026 position paper re-ran the released MLC model and reports per-episode success as low as 41%, 52% and 54% on its worst validation episodes, a systematic confusion of "twice" with "thrice" in one episode, and that 179 of 200 validation episodes use operation combinations already present in the 100,000 training episodes; it argues MLC succeeds only under a "very narrow and restricted definition of a meta-learning setup" — [Woydt et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01820)
- Rule synthesis as meta-learning (Nye et al. 2020) **[NP]**: LSTMs of size 200; MiniSCAN episodes use grammars with 3 to 4 primitive and 2 to 4 higher-order rules and 10 to 20 support examples; SCAN training saw about 9 million sampled grammars in 48 hours with 30 to 50 support examples. On the human few-shot task: 98.75% with search, 37.5% without search, meta seq2seq 25% — [Nye et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05562)
- Meta-learning without episodes of supports (Conklin et al., ACL 2021) **[NP]**: MAML where each "meta-test" batch is sampled to be similar to the "meta-train" batch (Levenshtein, string kernel or tree kernel over the top 1,000 neighbours). Models: hidden size 256, Transformer 2 + 2 layers. COGS Transformer 58.6 ± 3.7 to 66.7 ± 4.4 (tree kernel), LSTM 34.5 to 41.0; SCAN MCD1/2/3 LSTM 27.4 / 31.0 / 9.6 to 47.6 / 35.2 / 11.4, Transformer 2.6 / 3.1 / 2.3 to 4.7 / 6.7 / 6.5. Seed spread is large (best 73%, worst 54% for one configuration), and the authors say applying it "outside of synthetic datasets is important future work" — [Conklin et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04252)
- Meta-in-context training from scratch (Han and Pado 2024) **[NP]**: a causal Transformer (8 layers, 8 heads, d = 512, about 25.2M parameters) trained on sequences of M randomly ordered training pairs with loss on outputs only; at test time k = M - 1 random training pairs are the context. SCAN MCD1/2/3 21.8 / 25.6 / 19.7 (same model, M = 1) to 60.4 / 53.3 / 50.7, and 71.2 / 74.8 on MCD1/2 with output-vocabulary shuffling (the paper reports MCD3 with shuffling inconsistently as 38.7 and 50.7); COGS 51.9 to 75.7 (an ordinary 3-layer encoder-decoder gets 80); GeoQuery TMCD 37.4 to 40.8. With a pretrained GPT-2 **[PT]** the same regime gives SCAN MCD 84.7 / 71.5 / 69.1 and COGS 82.4. The paper notes "a conflict between memorization and in-context learning" and lists retrieval-based support selection as future work — [Han and Pado 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11834)
- Compositional-ARC (Mondorf et al., ICLR 2026) **[NP]**: MLC on grids; 3 + 3 layers, 8 heads, width 128, 5.7M parameters; 100,000 episodes (82,908 train), each with 12 study examples (6 primitive transformations, 6 two-way compositions) and 10 queries of unseen three-way compositions. Systematicity test: 78.26% versus 0.53% (o3-mini), 0.99% (GPT-4o), 2.66% (Gemini 2.0 Flash) and 78.20% for an 8B model with test-time training. Ablations (mean over splits): full 86.73, no copy task 69.05, no primitive examples 75.27, no composition examples 21.01. No ordinary supervised baseline is reported — [Mondorf et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01445)
- What makes a from-scratch Transformer read its context instead of memorising (Chan et al., NeurIPS 2022) **[NP, 12 layers, width 64, 831,479 parameters]**: in-context learning rises with burstiness of the training sequences (standard setting p(bursty) = 0.9), with the number of rare classes (100 to 1,600 to 12,800), with several labels per class and with within-class variation; "we need both burstiness and a large number of classes"; a Zipf exponent of about 1 is the only setting where in-context and in-weights learning coexist; otherwise more of one means less of the other. Matched RNNs and LSTMs never learned in context — [Chan et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05055)
- A review by Irie and Lake (Nature Machine Intelligence 2025) frames these results as giving networks "incentives and practice" for the skill (verified at abstract level only) — [Irie and Lake 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10596)

#### Evidence on natural language and on language-to-program tasks
- From-scratch meta-in-context training moves GeoQuery TMCD by 3.4 points (37.4 to 40.8), far less than on SCAN — [Han and Pado 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11834)
- Grounded instruction following with GPT-3.5-paraphrased instructions (NL-gSCAN split H): no supports 0.19, coverage retrieval 0.44, output-similarity retrieval 0.17, generated supports 0.59 — [Spilsbury et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13092)
- LAPS (section 1) is the nearest language-to-program evidence: it is not episodic meta-learning, but trains its search model on sampled program-description pairs and solves 54.6% / 89.2% / 97.4% of tasks described in human language after training on synthetic language — [Wong et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11053)

#### Small or from-scratch models that use retrieved exemplars
- ReCode (Hayati et al., EMNLP 2018) **[NP]**: retrieves training descriptions by word-level edit similarity (top 3 or 10), extracts n-gram action subtrees (up to 4-grams) from their syntax trees, rewrites copied variables through a word alignment, and adds a bonus (lambda = 3) to decoder actions that would complete a retrieved n-gram. Django accuracy 71.6 to 72.8, Hearthstone accuracy 16.2 to 19.6 and BLEU 75.8 to 78.4 — [Hayati et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10025)
- Retrieve-and-edit (Hashimoto et al., NeurIPS 2018) **[NP]**: a retriever trained so that the retrieved example's output is easy to edit into the gold output, plus an LSTM editor with copying. GitHub Python autocomplete BLEU 19.2 (seq2seq) to 34.7; with a lexical bag-of-words retriever 23.1; Hearthstone BLEU 60.4 to 70.0 and accuracy 1.5 to 9.1 (syntax-tree models reach 79.2 / 22.7) — [Hashimoto et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01194)
- Generate-and-retrieve (Zemlyanskiy et al. 2022) **[PT, T5]**: retrieving by similarity to the model's own preliminary output raises the share of cases where a top-4 exemplar has the gold template from 35.9 to 70.0 (MTOP bootstrap), 55.1 to 68.7 and 20.1 to 52.5 (TOPv2 settings); exact match improves by 0.1 to 1.5 points over input-similarity retrieval (for example 74.9 to 76.4 on new domains); retrieval adds little when training data are abundant (86.4 versus 86.4 on full MTOP) — [Zemlyanskiy et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14899)
- Covering local structures (Levy, Bogin, Berant, ACL 2023) **[PT]**: an auxiliary model predicts candidate programs, their sub-trees become the target set, and demonstrations are chosen greedily to cover them (largest first, one per template). Codex without fine-tuning on SMCalFlow-CS 8 / 16 / 32-shot cross-domain: top-K neighbours 34.0 / 35.7 / 50.3, Cover-LS 53.3 / 58.3 / 73.5, oracle Cover-LS 77.3 / 73.5 / 75.3. Fine-tuned T5-large with 3 demonstrations: no demonstrations 9.7 / 37.5 / 59.4; top-K at training and test 8.8 / 22.1 / 46.1 (and GeoQuery template 83.8 to 54.7); demonstrations that each cover one program symbol, chosen at random per symbol ("Cover-LS1"), 31.0 / 51.3 / 62.6; with oracle structures at test time 48.0 / 64.1 / 73.5. Cover-LS collapses (0.3) on the zero-shot split where the auxiliary model itself gets 0 — [Levy et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06800)
- Generating supports when retrieval cannot find them (Spilsbury, Marttinen, Ilin, EMNLP 2024) **[NP; 12 layers, width 512, 16 supports, 300,000 steps]**: gSCAN split H success: Transformer without supports 0.22, the same model fine-tuned on the generated demonstrations 0.22, random same-state supports 0.15, output-similarity retrieval 0.17 ± .01, coverage retrieval 0.56 ± .10, generated supports 0.80 ± .05, oracle supports 0.86. Generated supports cover the right verb in 1.00 and the right adverb in 0.88 of cases (retrieval 0.15 and 0.51); removing the adverb supports gives 0.30, the verb supports 0.21; only 56% of generated supports are correct. Two other splits needing unseen output patterns stay at 0.0 to 0.03 for every method — [Spilsbury et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13092); [ACL Anthology entry](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.893/)

### Inferences
- MLC's permutation episodes, LexSym's lexical swaps and Prim2PrimX's primitive clones are the same intervention seen from two sides: all make the model see every word class bound to many meanings. MLC adds the skill of reading the binding from context. For the target system the natural episode is: a query (description plus example) preceded by k short study items that jointly cover the operators of the target program (operator documentation phrases or small description-program pairs), with operator names or phrases permuted per episode with high probability so that memorising pairs cannot work. This addresses failure mode 3 (pair-by-pair memorisation) by design, in line with Chan et al.'s burstiness and many-rare-classes conditions.
- The same literature bounds what to expect: every meta-learned model above fails completely on structure it never practised (length, recursion, gSCAN splits D and G), so this route fixes lexical binding and reuse of demonstrated pieces, not unseen parent-to-child links by itself, unless the supports demonstrate the link.
- Failure mode 4 (a helpful relative in the top 5 for 15 of 77 tasks with word-overlap retrieval) matches the published pattern: input-similarity retrieval finds the right template in 20 to 55% of cases, and retrieval for split-H-type novelty succeeds in 0.17. The two published fixes are to retrieve by the content of the model's own candidate programs (operators and links in the beam) with a coverage objective, and to generate supports when the pool has none. This system can always generate a support for any operator or link from its templated generator, which is exactly the DemoGen situation.
- Training with supports must avoid the over-copying trap: nearest-neighbour supports at training time made a fine-tuned T5 worse than no supports on compositional splits (8.8 versus 9.7; 54.7 versus 83.8). Symbol-level coverage with a random example per symbol is the published remedy.
- Model sizes in this literature (1.4M to 25M) are compatible with a 12.4M coder; the cost is sequence length (k supports multiply input length by roughly k + 1), which matters on a 4-core CPU.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that trains a small from-scratch model to use retrieved exemplars for natural-language-to-program generation; the closest are ReCode and retrieve-and-edit (2018, modest gains), DemoGen (grounded instructions) and Levy et al. (pretrained T5/Codex).
- Evidence of transfer of MLC-style training to real language is limited to GeoQuery TMCD (+3.4) and GPT-paraphrased gSCAN; Lake and Baroni state the method is untested on natural language. I did not access the Nature supplementary material beyond the PMC full text (which was truncated near the end).
- Compositional-ARC reports no non-meta-learned supervised baseline, so the share of its accuracy due to the episodic format is unknown.
- I read only the first part of the Woydt et al. critique; its appendix may contain further experiments.

## 4. Architecture and output-representation choices that help small transformers (training details, relative positions, copy decoders, grammar and tree decoders, intermediate representations, execution decomposition, output order)

### Takeaway
For small from-scratch transformers the dependable cheap wins are training details, not architecture: relative positions, embedding scaling, no early stopping on in-distribution data and model selection by accuracy on a generalization set take COGS from 35% to 81% and PCFG productivity from 50% to 85% at under 10M parameters, and a copy decoder helps on average (0.346 to 0.403 over 12 tasks). Grammar-constrained decoders, tree decoders and coarse-to-fine sketches add a few in-distribution points but do not close compositional gaps (text-to-SQL program split 10.8 to 14.1). The largest reported gains in program synthesis come from changing what the decoder conditions on: step-by-step generation with execution feedback (RobustFill compositional average 42.4 to 80.2, and 87.0 with predicted subgoals; DeepCoder 5.7 to 18.1 to 23.1) and sketch-plus-search (held-out concept 1.1 to 29.8).

### Cited Findings

#### Training details, positions, size and depth
- Csordas, Irie, Schmidhuber (EMNLP 2021) **[NP]**: COGS 0.35 (original setup with early stopping) to 0.65 by disabling early stopping alone, to 0.81 ± 0.01 with position-embedding downscaling (PED), no label smoothing and a fixed learning rate of 1e-4; one seed change in the original setup gave 2.5%. PCFG productivity 0.50 to 0.65 by training about ten times longer, 0.85 with a Universal Transformer plus relative positions; PCFG systematicity 0.72 to 0.96. SCAN length (cutoff 26) 0.30 absolute positions, 0.72 relative, 1.00 universal plus relative. CFQ MCD mean stays at 0.20 for all four variants; CFQ output-length split 0.57 to 0.81. Embedding scaling on COGS / PCFG: standard up-scaling 0.78 / 0.47, none 0.62 / 0.63, PED 0.80 / 0.65. Parameter counts: SCAN 0.33M to 1.1M, COGS 5.1M to 10.3M — [Csordas et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12284)
- Their recommendations: relative positions by default; do not select models by early stopping on the in-distribution split (its accuracy is near 100% for every model); "use the accuracy instead of the loss" for stopping and tuning; build a generalization validation set — [Csordas et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12284)
- Ontanon et al. (ACL 2022) **[NP; d = 64 or 128, 2 to 6 layers]**: average over 12 tasks 0.137 (absolute positions) to 0.346 (relative embeddings and biases, also in decoder-to-encoder attention) to 0.403 with a copy decoder, 0.479 with 4 layers, 0.527 for the best shared-weight model. Per task: addition 0.005 to 0.988; PCFG productivity 0.174 to 0.634; PCFG systematicity 0.434 to 0.828; COGS 0.177 to 0.520 (copy decoder) to 0.784 when recast as sequence tagging (the paper's tagging table lists the absolute-position seq2seq baseline as 0.278, so its tables use two setups); CFQ MCD1 0.304 to 0.327, and 0.555 with an intermediate representation; SCAN add-jump 0.003 to 0.017 (unsolved); a copy decoder slightly lowers CFQ (0.295). Six unshared layers hurt (averages 0.375 / 0.398); weight sharing helps PCFG but hurts COGS. Relative encodings with embeddings and biases "seem to never be detrimental" — [Ontanon et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.04378)
- Model size on COGS (Kim and Linzen 2020) **[NP]**: 9.5M-parameter Transformer 0.35 ± 0.06 on the generalization set, a 45M model 0.20 ± 0.26, a roughly 5M model 0.37 ± 0.14; in-distribution accuracy 96 to 99% for all; seed sensitivity ± 6 to 8 points — [Kim and Linzen 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05465)
- Depth (Petty et al., NAACL 2024): language models pre-trained by the authors on C4 **[PT, self-pretrained]** at fixed parameter counts (41M, 134M, 374M): COGS 12.4% with 1 layer to 58.2% with 2 layers (41M); gains saturate at about 4 to 6 layers on COGS and 2 to 4 on GeoQuery; depth helps lexical, not structural, generalization — [Petty et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19956)
- A 2025/2026 theory-and-experiment paper finds the data needed for a two-hop composition grows as about n^2.26 in the number of tokens n and that this exponent is unchanged from 68M to 1.5B parameters ("primarily determined by data properties rather than model capacity or architectures") — [Chang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20278)
- A 2026 preprint on small from-scratch decoder-only models (adapted SCAN) reports that depth "has minimal effect" on out-of-distribution accuracy and that a regulariser aligning hidden-state composition with syntax improves it (p = 0.023; effect size not in the abstract) — [An and Du 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18858)
- Pretraining is not a substitute: T5-small without pretraining gets CFQ MCD 21.4, with pretraining 28.0, T5-11B 40.9; on SCAN length pretraining lowers accuracy (19.4 to 10.9) — [Furrer et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08970)

#### Copy, pointer and attention-supervision mechanisms
- Attention-based copying: GeoQuery 74.6 to 85.0, ATIS 69.9 to 76.3, Overnight 76.7 to 75.8 **[NP]** — [Jia and Liang 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03622)
- On COGS an LSTM goes 0.51 (no copy) to 0.66 (copy) to 0.82 (lexicon translation channel) **[NP]**; in the recombination model of Akyurek et al., removing copying takes SCAN jump from 0.88 to 0.00 — [Akyurek and Andreas 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03993); [Akyurek et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03706)
- Text-to-SQL program (compositional) split, average over ATIS, GeoQuery, Advising and Scholar (Oren et al. 2020), iid / program accuracy: seq2seq with GloVe 74.9 / 10.8; with attention supervision from automatic alignments 73.3 / 18.5; with a coverage mechanism 75.3 / 17.0; ELMo **[PT embeddings]** plus coverage 76.2 / 24.1; plus attention supervision 72.0 / 25.4. The authors conclude the result "leaves much to be desired" and that "more drastic changes" may be needed — [Oren et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05647)
- Span-level attention supervision (sub-programs aligned to utterance spans via GIZA++) with a BERT encoder **[PT]**: SMCalFlow-CS cross-skill 33.6 to 46.8 at 16 examples, 53.5 to 61.7 at 32, 71.3 to 74.3 at 128; token-level supervision 39.7 at 16 — [Yin et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.225/)

#### Grammar-constrained decoders, tree decoders and sketches
- TranX (Yin and Neubig 2018) **[NP per the paper's description; pretraining not mentioned]**: a transition system over an ASDL grammar (ApplyConstr, Reduce, GenToken; top-down, left-to-right). In-distribution accuracy: GeoQuery 88.2 (87.7 with parent feeding), ATIS 86.2, Django 73.7 (72.7 without parent feeding; earlier syntactic model 71.6), WikiSQL 62.9 exact match. Abstract Syntax Networks with supervised attention are listed at GeoQuery 87.1 and ATIS 85.9 — [Yin and Neubig 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02720)
- Coarse-to-fine decoding (Dong and Lapata, ACL 2018) **[NP except GloVe on WikiSQL]**: first a sketch with arguments and names abstracted away, then the details. GeoQuery 85.0 to 88.2, ATIS 85.3 to 87.7, Django 69.5 to 74.1, WikiSQL 68.8 to 71.7; with oracle sketches 93.9 / 95.1 / 83.0 / 73.0 — [Dong and Lapata 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04793)
- Grammar decoding alone does not fix compositional splits: text-to-SQL average iid / program 70.1 / 14.1 for a grammar-based decoder versus 74.9 / 10.8 for seq2seq; in another study a grammar decoder gets GeoQuery template 54.0 versus 46.0 for seq2seq, and 0.0 / 4.2 on SCAN right / around-right — [Oren et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05647); [Herzig and Berant 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06040)
- On SMCalFlow-CS a coarse-to-fine decoder with a BERT encoder **[PT]** gets 40.6 / 54.6 / 65.7 / 72.9 versus 33.6 / 53.5 / 64.2 / 71.3 for plain BERT2Seq at 16 / 32 / 64 / 128 cross-skill examples — [Yin et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.225/)
- Description plus examples to program on AlgoLisp (Polosukhin and Skidanov 2018) **[NP, hidden size 100]**: 79,214 training tasks; attentional seq2seq 54.1 (test), tree decoder 61.0, tree decoder plus tree-beam search that executes candidates on 3 of the task's examples 85.8; search without the model 0.6; examples only, no description, 2.2 — [Polosukhin and Skidanov 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04335)
- SketchAdapt (Nye et al., ICML 2019) **[NP; generator about 7M, recognizer about 230k parameters]**: a neural generator writes a sketch with holes and an enumerator guided by a small recognizer fills them; the training target is the most likely sketch whose holes the enumerator can fill within a sampled time budget. AlgoLisp test 90.0 (generator only 88.6, enumerator only 7.3). When all tasks containing the concept "odd" are removed from training (8,000 programs), accuracy on "even" / "odd" test tasks is 34.4 / 29.8 for SketchAdapt, 4.5 / 1.1 for the generator alone and 23.7 / 0.0 for the enumerator alone — [Nye et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06349)
- Sketch-driven regular-expression synthesis from language and examples (Ye et al., TACL 2020) **[NP]**: on Turk (10,000 synthetic-language pairs) seq2seq 58.2, seq2seq filtered by the examples 82.8 to 84.3, sketch parser plus example-guided synthesizer 84.3 to 86.2; on KB13 (824 pairs) 65.6 / 77.7 to 80.1 / 82.5 to 86.4. On 62 real StackOverflow posts a transferred seq2seq with example filtering gets 0% while the sketch approach gets 31.1% top-1 and 54.1% top-5. Even with 20 examples per task, 92.8% of filtered seq2seq outputs are consistent with the examples but only 84.3% are correct — [Ye et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05848)
- DeepCoder (Balog et al., ICLR 2017) **[NP; feed-forward, 3 layers of 256 units]**: predicting only which functions occur in the program (34 independent presence probabilities, from 5 examples) and letting enumerative search do the arrangement gives speed-ups of 62.2x (length-3 programs) and 907x (length 5, network trained on length 4) for sort-and-add enumeration at the 20% solved mark; the authors summarise "1-3 orders" of magnitude and report generalization across program lengths — [Balog et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01989)

#### Intermediate representations and output format
- Herzig et al. (2021) **[PT]**: reversible and lossy intermediate representations take T5-base from 34.6 to 67.8 on CFQ MCD (T5-3B 49.5 to 83.8); the largest single effect is grouping conjuncts with a shared subject and relation (60.8 to 38.7 when removed). For a T5-small **without pretraining** the same representations give only 20.8 to 22.6 on CFQ and hurt text-to-SQL (33.7 to 21.9) — [Herzig et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07478)
- Conflicting from-scratch evidence **[NP]**: a small Transformer on CFQ MCD1 goes 0.304 to 0.555 with an intermediate representation, COGS as tagging 0.784 versus 0.278 as seq2seq; a Transformer on simplified SPARQL is listed at CFQ MCD mean 31.4 versus 17.9 — [Ontanon et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.04378); [Jambor and Bahdanau 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07572)
- ReCOGS (Wu, Manning, Potts, TACL 2023) **[NP; LSTM about 9M, Transformer about 4M]**: incidental details of the target format drive results. Removing redundant tokens: LSTM overall 32.1 to 39.6, Transformer 81.3 to 83.6, one lexical case 62.4 to 81.1. Adding training sentences whose subject noun phrase carries a modifier (participial verb phrases, "easy" variant) takes object-PP-to-subject-PP from 0.0 to 88.7 (LSTM) and 82.7 (Transformer). Recursion failures are "largely due to the fact that models are not trained with longer sequences", and models exploit position-based variable names — [Wu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13716)
- The decoder, not the encoder, is the bottleneck for structure: probes on BART's encoder recover 82 to 92% on COGS structural types where the decoder scores 0; "structural generalization is hard only if the decoder's task is sufficiently complex" — [Yao and Koller 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13050)
- Re-encoding the source at every decoding step (Dangle): Transformer with GloVe embeddings on COGS 85.5 to 85.9; RoBERTa **[PT]** on CFQ 43.4 to 66.1; cost rises from O(n^2 + m^2) to O(m(n^2 + m^2)) — [Zheng and Lapata 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04655)
- Output order in a 10.6M-parameter from-scratch Transformer (NanoGPT: 6 layers, 6 heads, width 384): 3-digit addition written most-significant-digit first plateaus at about 85 to 87% with 10,000 samples; written in reverse (the order in which the computation proceeds) it reaches 100% at about 2,500 samples; with a detailed scratchpad of intermediate steps at 1,000 samples — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Making intermediate results explicit lowers the data-scaling exponent of a three-hop composition from 2.58 to 1.76 — [Chang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20278)

#### Decomposition by execution and bottom-up construction
- Shi et al. (2022) **[NP; 3 layers, embedding 256, hidden 512]** define program-synthesis generalization tasks (length, compose different concepts, switch concept order, compose new operation, add operation functionality). Zero-shot, a plain Transformer gets 10.5 on SCAN compose-different-concepts, relative attention 29.2, relative attention with subprogram separators and an attention mask 74.4; on RobustFill switch-concept-order every variant stays between 4.7 and 6.6 — [Shi et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03758)
- ExeDec (Shi et al., ICLR 2024) **[NP; 3 layers, embedding 512, hidden 1024, 500K steps of fresh synthetic data]**: one model predicts the execution subgoal (the values the next subprogram should produce), a second writes a subprogram that reaches it, the subprogram is executed and the specification updated. End-to-end accuracy averaged over the five generalization tasks, beam 10: RobustFill plain Transformer 42.4, step-by-step with execution but no subgoal prediction 80.2, ExeDec 87.0; DeepCoder 5.7 / 18.1 / 23.1. Without distribution shift: RobustFill 90.9 / 94.9 / 95.5; DeepCoder 53.6 / 64.7 / 61.2. Stated limit: it "needs a training dataset with ground-truth decompositions". The same prompting style helps a large LLM only modestly (6.2 to 16.2 of 200 solved) — [Shi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13883)
- LambdaBeam (Shi et al., NeurIPS 2023) **[NP; about 13M parameters, about 6.5M synthetic training tasks]**: bottom-up search in which every explored sub-expression is executed on the examples and its value embedded; lambdas are embedded by running them on 16 canonical argument tuples; 23 first-order and 5 higher-order operations. Solves 67.2 of 100 handwritten tasks in 10 minutes each, 24% more than the best baseline; "on-policy training is slow" (a week on 8 GPUs) and the "DSL is small" — [Shi et al. 2023b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02049)
- A 2025 controlled study on ARC-like tasks (34-primitive DSL, 14 training task types, 7 held-out compositions) **[NP for the main model; 4 + 4 layers, d = 256]**: execution-guided synthesis with search 80% success, the same network without search 10%, a non-execution-guided neural search 42.86%, test-time fine-tuning of a from-scratch 0.5B model 10% (35.71% with augmentations; 90% when starting from pretrained weights) — [Ouellette 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15877)

### Inferences
- A cheap checklist for the 12.4M coder follows directly from Csordas et al. and Ontanon et al.: relative position encodings in encoder and decoder, PED-style embedding scaling, no label smoothing, checkpoint selection by accuracy on a held-out novel-link set (not loss, not in-distribution accuracy), a copy or lexical channel, and no extra unshared depth beyond about 4 to 6 layers. On benchmarks these changes are worth tens of points on lexical and length generalization, but CFQ-type compound novelty stays at about 20%, so they should be treated as hygiene, not as the fix for failure mode 1.
- The program grammar and types are known in this system, so typed constrained decoding is free; the literature says to expect only a few points from it on compositional splits. It removes ill-typed candidates, which are not the ones that cause failure mode 2 (wrong but type-valid programs that fit the example).
- Prefix (top-down) output makes each token's main context its parent token, which is exactly the statistic that fails. The execution-decomposition results suggest changing the conditioning rather than the model: build the expression inside-out, execute each completed sub-expression on the single example, and feed the resulting value (type and content) back as the context for choosing the next operator. Many different child sub-programs produce the same kind of value, so a parent operator that has been seen applied to "a list of integers" is covered even if the specific parent-to-child token pair is new. The ExeDec ablation shows most of the gain (42.4 to 80.2) comes from this step-by-step execution, not from subgoal prediction, and all 300k synthetic programs can supply ground-truth decompositions for free.
- Caveats for that transfer: the published systems condition on several input-output examples and no description; with one example, intermediate values are less discriminating, and a wrong early step is not recoverable without search.
- Sketch-first decoding has large measured headroom (oracle sketches add 6 to 9 points in-distribution) and the best out-of-distribution evidence (held-out concept 1.1 to 29.8) when holes are filled by search and the training sketches are chosen by what the search can fill within budget. Ye et al.'s 0% versus 31% on real StackOverflow descriptions suggests that sketch-plus-search degrades more gracefully than end-to-end decoding when wording shifts.
- DeepCoder-style prediction of the bag of operators is the simplest lexical head: it involves no links at all, and the system's enumerative engine can do the arranging.
- The regex result (92.8% consistent with 20 examples, 84.3% correct) quantifies the floor of failure mode 2: even many examples leave 8 to 9% spurious programs, so with one example a ranking or prior term is unavoidable.

### Gaps
- No study found compares prefix, postfix or execution-ordered linearizations for natural-language-to-program generation under compositional splits with a from-scratch model; the output-order evidence comes from arithmetic and from examples-only program synthesis.
- ExeDec's per-task numbers are only in a figure; the averages above are from its Table 2.
- The depth study uses pretrained language models; from-scratch depth effects are only covered by Ontanon et al. and Kim and Linzen.
- TranX, abstract syntax networks and coarse-to-fine decoding report in-distribution accuracy only; their effect on compositional splits is known only indirectly through Oren et al. (2020) and Yin et al. (2021).

## 5. Diagnostics and data selection (unobserved local structures, compound divergence, structurally diverse training sets, diverse demonstrations): does covering structures fix generalization?

### Takeaway
The diagnostics literature confirms the "seen-combination law" (unobserved parent-child or sibling structures predict failure with AUC 76 to 93; compound divergence explains accuracy with R^2 0.81 to 0.88; inputs outside coverage stay at chance however long training runs) and adds two qualifications: an unseen structure is easy when a similar one, the same shape with one symbol replaced by a distributionally similar symbol, has been seen, and a structure must be witnessed in several different contexts, not once. Covering structures largely fixes synthetic-language generalization (SCAN jump 3.5 to 99.7; COVR 19.7 to 92.6; template-uniform sampling doubles Schema2QA compositional accuracy with 200 times less data), but on natural language it recovers only part of the gap (Overnight 8.4 to 38.6 against 54.2 on random template splits; GeoQuery +3 to +5), and more data of the same kind does not close it.

### Cited Findings

#### Unobserved local structures (Bogin, Gupta, Berant, EMNLP 2022)
- Definition: the program is a tree with extra edges between consecutive siblings; a local structure is a connected subgraph of 2 to 4 nodes (2-LS = parent-child pairs and consecutive-sibling pairs). Hypothesis: a test instance is hard if it contains a local structure not observed in training, "only if there are no observed structures that are similar" — [Bogin et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05899)
- Easiness score: for each unobserved structure take the best similarity to any observed structure, then take the minimum over the instance's unobserved structures. Two structures are similar only if they are isomorphic and differ in at most one node label; the similarity is then the average Jaccard overlap of the two symbols' context sets (parents, children, left and right siblings). Example from the paper: "exists" versus "most" 75% — [Bogin et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05899)
- Predicting which test instances fail (AUC, 2-LS / 3-LS / 4-LS): COVR 93.3 / 93.1 / 92.1; Overnight synthetic wording 84.6 / 91.0 / 88.0; Overnight natural paraphrases 67.9 / 74.8 / 78.4; Schema2QA 78.9 / 81.6 / 79.9; ATIS 75.8 / 78.7 / 81.4; a plain token-bigram baseline 88.5 on COVR; a program-length baseline 49.7 (chance). Models are fine-tuned T5-Base/Large and BART-Base/Large **[PT]**; an LSTM decoder with a BERT-Base encoder gives AUC 80.0 to 82.6 on COVR and 56.8 to 60.9 on Schema2QA. At least 3 of the 4 models agree on 93.1 to 96.4% of instances — [Bogin et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05899)
- Split-level: correlation between the 2-LS measure and exact match across splits is 0.79 on COVR (TMCD-style compound divergence 0.36) and 0.84 on Overnight (0.60) — [Bogin et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05899)
- Adversarial splits that hold out one 2-LS together with all structures similar to it: exact match 32.8 ± 43.6 on COVR and 23.1 ± 33.6 on Overnight; ignoring sibling relations 19.7 and 8.4; random template splits 94.6 and 54.2. When only half of the similar structures are held out, "meaning some similar structures will exist in the training set", accuracy returns to 92.6 on COVR but only to 38.6 on Overnight — [Bogin et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05899)
- Token-level: when a model's first wrong token is examined, it involves an unobserved 2-LS in 76.5% of cases on COVR but only 28.4% (Overnight), 31.8% (Schema2QA) and 45.1% (ATIS). The authors attribute lower predictability on natural-language data to "ambiguity, lexical alignment and evaluation errors" — [Bogin et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05899)
- Training-set selection: repeatedly pick a not-yet-observed local structure and add a random pool example containing it; this "outperforms or matches the random sampling method on almost all datasets and training budgets" (COVR, ATIS, Schema2QA; numbers only in a figure) — [Bogin et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05899)

#### Compound divergence and the effect of scale
- CFQ (Keysers et al., ICLR 2020): atoms are generation rules, compounds are sub-graphs of rule applications; atom divergence uses a Chernoff coefficient with alpha = 0.5, compound divergence with alpha = 0.1; MCD splits maximise compound divergence (0.69 to 0.71) with atom divergence at most 0.02. Accuracy random split versus MCD mean **[NP]**: LSTM with attention 97.4 versus 14.9, Transformer 98.5 versus 17.9, Universal Transformer 98.0 versus 18.9; on SCAN 99.9 versus 6.1, 100.0 versus 1.1, 99.9 versus 1.2. R^2 between compound divergence and accuracy 0.81 to 0.88, versus 0.11 to 0.22 for length ratios. All models stay above 95% on a random split with 10 times less training data — [Keysers et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09713)
- *-CFQ (Tsarkov et al. 2021) **[NP Transformer]**: with training sets from about 10k to nearly 900k examples, "compositional generalization remains a challenge at all training sizes"; at compound divergence 0.2 the error stays "an order of magnitude higher" than at divergence 0 even at the largest sizes; error follows a rough power law in data size; widening the rule scope raises the error plateau ("roughly 3 times" for the largest variant) and "training data alone is insufficient to fully offset" it; extra data from related domains helps mainly when target data are scarce and helps reliably only when at least half of the training mix is from the target domain — [Tsarkov et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08266)
- Coverage theory (Chang et al. 2025; revised 2026 under the title "Characterizing Pattern Matching and Its Limits on Compositional Task Structures") **[NP GPT-2-style and Mamba models]**: two fragments are functionally k-equivalent if they behave identically in at least k shared contexts; the k-coverage is everything reachable from training inputs by substituting equivalent fragments. Accuracy outside coverage "remains at chance level (about 1/50) regardless of training time"; a single witness (k = 1) is "practically insufficient", and test accuracy rises with the number of witnessing contexts. Data needed for perfect in-distribution two-hop generalization scales as n^c with c between 2 and 2.5 in theory and 2.26 measured (2.43 parallel two-hop, 2.58 three-hop), unchanged across a 20-fold parameter range. When one variable reaches the output by two paths the models fail to form a unified intermediate representation even at 0.96 in-distribution accuracy. The authors suggest "targeted data augmentation to maximize coverage" — [Chang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20278)

#### Choosing or generating training examples
- Sampling synthetic training data uniformly over abstract program templates (Oren et al. 2021) **[PT, BART-base]**: compositional exact match 41.4 with 5K examples versus 37.7 for 1M uniformly sampled examples and 20.1 with none; gains plateau (43.0 at 120K); a compound-maximum-entropy sampler reaches 40.2; in-distribution accuracy is 81.2 without synthetic data, 83.4 with 5K template-uniform examples and 84.4 with 120K (78.9 with 5K uniformly sampled examples) — [Oren et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02575)
- Structurally diverse sampling (Gupta, Singh, Gardner 2022) **[PT, BART-base]**: selecting training sets for diversity of program sub-trees beats random sampling in 9 of 10 dataset-split combinations and gives roughly 2x sample efficiency on template splits; diversity of whole templates or bigrams often falls below random on in-distribution splits unless frequent structures are prioritised (exact accuracies only in figures) — [Gupta et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08445)
- Diverse demonstrations (Levy et al. 2023) **[PT]**: covering predicted local structures instead of taking nearest neighbours raises Codex accuracy on SMCalFlow-CS 8-shot cross-domain from 34.0 to 53.3 and on GeoQuery TMCD from 71.8 to 79.4; the method depends on an auxiliary model that can predict the right structures (0.3 on the zero-shot split where it cannot) — [Levy et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06800)
- More primitives (Patel et al., ACL 2022) **[NP]**: adding more example primitives and their uses to SCAN-like training data gives "a clear trend of improvement"; with 100 example primitives a Transformer reaches 94.5% on a test with 10 isolated primitives; skewed usage (10% of primitives with 500 examples each, most with one) removes the benefit; larger models overfit more; after pre-training on many primitives, fine-tuning all parameters on original SCAN generalises "very poorly" while freezing encoder and decoder keeps it near perfect (most numbers only in figures) — [Patel et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07402)
- Data factors (Zhou, Jiang, Bansal, EMNLP 2023) **[NP; 3-layer encoder-decoder, width 256]**: primitive augmentation at 2x / 20x / 200x gives SCAN jump 3.49 to 77.37 / 99.68 / 99.93 but GeoQuery query split 42.37 to 45.92 / 47.85 / 45.70. Repetition hurts: duplicating examples lowers GeoQuery query split from 42.37 to 24.84, and concentrating repetition on few primitives lowers it to 1.65 (20x data: 47.85 / 44.95 / 31.40). Training on 25% subsets: ATIS simple 40.86, hard 52.26, mixed 48.11; SMCalFlow-CS simple 36.72, hard 38.43, mixed 39.41. The two stated mechanisms are that more diverse patterns make memorisation harder and that lower frequency of any one example discourages it — [Zhou et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04420)
- Exposure count (Kim and Linzen 2020) **[NP]**: raising the number of exposure examples per new primitive from 1 to 100 lifts COGS generalization from 0.35 to 0.63 (Transformer) and 0.16 to 0.50 (BiLSTM), on lexical cases only; structural cases stay at or near zero — [Kim and Linzen 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05465)
- Synthetic program distributions (Shin et al., ICLR 2019) **[NP]**: equalising the distribution of salient variables (program length, nesting, control-flow ratio, input features) in generated training data raises accuracy on shifted test sets from 0.04 to 24.30% up to 62.78 to 80.19% while staying close to the baseline on the original test set (73.52 versus 71.8 in one augmentation experiment); the salient variables are hand-chosen — [Shin et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12345)

### Inferences
- The target system's law is Bogin et al.'s 2-LS rule with similarity set to zero. Their refinement is directly testable on existing logs: among tasks with one unseen parent-to-child link, compare those where a similar link was seen (same parent with a child of the same type signature and similar contexts, or vice versa) with those where none was. If accuracy does not differ, the coder is not exploiting substitutability between same-type operators at all, and making it do so (type-tied operator embeddings, a type-then-operator factorised output, or class-level swaps in the data) is the first lever. If it does differ, link coverage should be planned at the level of operator classes, which is the only level at which it is affordable.
- Token-level coverage is not affordable here. With about 780 operators there are several hundred thousand possible ordered pairs, each needing several witnessing contexts, and the measured scaling exponent (about 2.3 in vocabulary size, unchanged by a 20-fold larger model) says more parameters will not help. Coverage has to come through equivalence classes (types, argument roles) or through explicit intermediate values (section 4).
- For generating synthetic programs the consistent advice is: sample uniformly over abstract templates in which operator names are replaced by their classes; cap how often any operator or skeleton repeats; mix easy and hard programs; equalise a few salient variables (depth, operator category, input type). Random program sampling over-represents a few structures, which is what the 200x efficiency gap measures.
- Coverage is necessary but, on natural language, not sufficient. In Bogin et al.'s own data, only 28 to 45% of first errors on natural-language sets involve an unseen local structure, natural-wording predictability is 17 points of AUC lower than synthetic wording for the same programs, and restoring similar structures recovers Overnight only to 38.6. The residual is lexical grounding ("linking errors"), which matches failure mode 2 and 3 of the target system: links taught with templated words do not become usable under natural wording.
- The same selection logic applies to the LLM-teacher budget: choose which synthetic programs the teacher describes by coverage of local structures and operators that natural data have not yet witnessed several times, not by skeleton frequency.

### Gaps
- Bogin et al.'s predictor was validated only on pretrained models and an LSTM with a BERT encoder; I found no replication with fully from-scratch models, and no test under verification by input-output examples.
- Exact gains for coverage-driven training-set selection (Bogin et al.) and structurally diverse sampling (Gupta et al.) exist only in figures that the text does not quantify; Patel et al.'s numbers are likewise mostly in figures.
- The CFQ appendix on training-set size was beyond the part of the page I could read.
- No study found reports how many witnessing contexts per structure are enough for a small seq2seq model on a realistic program vocabulary; the k-evidence results come from synthetic two-hop tasks with 50 tokens.
